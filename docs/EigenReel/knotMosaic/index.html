What you're seeing

Every tile shows one of eleven pictures: nothing, a straight piece, a quarter circle, a crossing, or two quarter circles. Lay them so the pieces meet edge to edge and a closed curve appears, with a crossing on some tiles. Lomonaco and Kauffman showed that every knot can be drawn this way.

A crossing says which strand goes over, so the drawing really describes a curve in space: push each over strand up and each under strand down. Then the curve lifts off and relaxes downhill on a knot energy (O'Hara's), which charges every two points of the curve 1/r⁴ for being a distance r apart in space, beyond what their distance along the curve already forces. Strands push each other apart but can never pass through each other, so the knot can't change; it just finds a rounder shape.

The name comes from the Jones polynomial. Each crossing can be smoothed two ways, and smoothing a crossing tile simply swaps it for one of the two-arc tiles. Kauffman's bracket adds up all 2c ways of smoothing the c crossings, and the polynomial tells these knots apart, even a knot from its mirror image.
