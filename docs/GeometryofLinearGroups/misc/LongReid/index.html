Integral Points on the Magnus Curve

Hunting infinite-order integer matrices in \(\left\langle \begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} t^2+1 & 2 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix} \right\rangle\) at algebraic values of \(t\)

📈 The Magnus Curve

The Magnus curve is the one-parameter family of two-generator matrix groups

\[ \Gamma_t \;=\; \langle A, B \rangle, \qquad A = \begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} t^2 + 1 & 2 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \]

Since \(\det A = t\) and \(\det B = (t-1)^2\), the entries of every word \(w(A,B)\) lie in the ring \(\mathbb{Z}\big[t,\, t^{-1},\, (t-1)^{-2}\big]\). For an algebraic specialization \(t = \tau\) with minimal polynomial \(m\) of degree \(d\), the group \(\Gamma_\tau\) sits inside \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}(\tau))\) — and if \(\tau\) and \(\tau - 1\) are units in the ring of integers (checked by \(|m(0)| = |m(1)| = 1\)), it sits inside \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\tau)})\).

The problem

For which algebraic \(\tau\) does \(\Gamma_\tau\) contain a matrix with all four entries in \(\mathbb{Z}\) — a rational integer point — of infinite order? Find the words \(w\) that produce them.

The curve comes from Magnus's analysis of the \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) character variety of the orbifold group \(\langle x, y \mid [x,y]^2 \rangle\) (W. Magnus, The uses of 2 by 2 matrices in combinatorial group theory, 1981): taking \(A\) diagonal, the commutator has trace zero iff \(2 B_{11} B_{22} = (t + t^{-1}) B_{12} B_{21}\), and the displayed \(B\) is the normalized solution. Long and Reid (1999) proposed specializing this curve to build a proper action of a surface group on a product of trees; the integral points hunted here are precisely the obstructions to properness — see the Long–Reid action section below, and N. Brody, An improper surface group action (arXiv:2512.19760), which settles the \(t = 9\) case. The flavor follows Long–Reid's Integral points on character varieties.

🌳 The Long–Reid Action at \(t = 9\)

For real \(t > 1\) the representation is faithful (one constructs an explicit hyperbolic rectangle realizing the \(T^2(2)\) structure), so \(\Gamma_t\) is honestly a surface group acting on \(\mathbb{H}^2\). When \(t\) is moreover a rational square, the generators normalize into \(\mathrm{PSL}_2\): at \(t = 9\),

\[ \Gamma \;=\; \left\langle \tfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},\; \tfrac{1}{8}\begin{pmatrix} 82 & 2 \\ 9 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \right\rangle \;\le\; \mathrm{PSL}_2\!\big(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac16]\big). \]

\(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[\frac16])\) is a lattice in \(\mathbb{H}^2 \times T_3 \times T_4\) (Borel–Harish-Chandra), where \(T_k\) is the \(k\)-regular Bruhat–Tits tree of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) over \(\mathbb{Q}_2, \mathbb{Q}_3\). The vertex stabilizer is \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), so:

Properness criterion

The action of \(\Gamma\) on \(T_3 \times T_4\) is proper \(\iff\) \(\Gamma \cap \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) is finite \(\iff\) the group contains no infinite-order integer matrix. A surface group acting properly on a product of trees would answer a well-known question in geometric group theory (cf. Fisher–Larsen–Spatzier–Stover, Character varieties and actions on products of trees, Israel J. Math. 2018, and Schwartz's notes on the Long–Reid conjecture).

arXiv:2512.19760 shows the action is not proper: the length-82 word (in our alphabet, lowercase = inverse)

AABaabABAAbabAABaBAAbabABAbABaBAAbAAABaab
AbaaBBAbaaBabAbaaBAbAABaBAbABaBAAbAAABaab

evaluates to an infinite-order matrix in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) with 25-digit entries (trace \(\approx 6.06 \times 10^{23}\)) — verified here in exact arithmetic by python/longReidBeam.py and stored in the database under t-9. The same script beam-searches for further witnesses; notably, plain beams stall in the torsion valley (the relator floods short words evaluating to \(\pm I\), and greedy denominator-descent circles them), so finding witnesses of this kind requires more structured searches.

🔄 Integrality = Galois Collision

Write \(\rho_\tau : F_2 = \langle \mathsf{A}, \mathsf{B}\rangle \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}(\tau))\) for the specialized representation. If \(\tau = \tau_1, \ldots, \tau_d\) are the Galois conjugates of \(\tau\), then applying a field embedding to the entries of \(\rho_\tau(w)\) gives \(\rho_{\tau_i}(w)\). Hence:

Collision criterion

\(\rho_\tau(w)\) has rational entries \(\iff\) the \(d\) Galois-conjugate specializations coincide on \(w\):

\[ \rho_{\tau_1}(w) = \rho_{\tau_2}(w) = \cdots = \rho_{\tau_d}(w). \]

The rational-entry words form a subgroup \(\Gamma_\tau \cap M_2(\mathbb{Q})\); when \(\tau, \tau-1\) are units the entries are algebraic integers, so rational entries are automatically rational integers.

So integral points are simultaneous collisions of \(d\) representations — the direct analogue of the collision searches used for Burau kernel words in braidGroups. The higher the degree of \(\tau\), the more conjugates must agree and the rarer the hits: our searches find rich families at degree 2 and, so far, only torsion at degree 3 and 4.

♻️ Universal Torsion: \([A,B]^2 = -I\)

The curve is not a curve of free groups. A direct computation shows that, identically in \(t\),

\[ \operatorname{tr}[A,B] = 0, \qquad \det[A,B] = 1, \]

so Cayley–Hamilton forces \([A,B]^2 = -I\) for every specialization. The image of \(\Gamma_t\) in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) is therefore a representation of the orbifold group \(\pi_1\big(T^2(2)\big) = \langle x, y \mid [x,y]^2 \rangle\) — a torus with a single cone point of order 2.

Consequently \(-I \in \Gamma_\tau\) for every \(\tau\), and every conjugate and cyclic rotation of \((\mathsf{ABab})^2\) is an integer matrix of finite order. These account for all the short integral words at many specializations (e.g. all 72 hits of length \(\le 12\) at the golden ratio are \(-I\)), which is why the database flags infinite_order separately: torsion hits are unavoidable background, infinite-order hits are the quarry.

✨ A Length-2 Miracle: the \(t + t^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}\) Family

The single most productive observation so far: the length-two word \(\mathsf{Ba} = BA^{-1}\) evaluates to

\[ BA^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} t + t^{-1} & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det BA^{-1} = (t + t^{-1}) - 2, \quad \operatorname{tr} BA^{-1} = (t + t^{-1}) + 1. \]

All entries are rational as soon as \(t + t^{-1} = n \in \mathbb{Z}\), i.e. whenever \(\tau\) is a root of \(t^2 - nt + 1\). This gives an infinite family of quadratic units with the integer point \(\big(\begin{smallmatrix} n & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\big)\) of determinant \(n - 2\) — infinite order for every \(n \ge 3\):

  • \(n = 3\), \(\tau = \varphi^2 = \frac{3+\sqrt 5}{2}\): the point \(\big(\begin{smallmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\big)\) is a hyperbolic element of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) — both \(\tau\) and \(\tau - 1 = \varphi\) are units, and the beam search finds its powers, negatives, and conjugates and (so far) nothing else.
  • \(n = 4\), \(\tau = 2 + \sqrt 3\): \(\big(\begin{smallmatrix} 4 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\big)\), determinant 2.
  • \(n \ge 5\): determinant \(n - 2 \ge 3\), still infinite order — integer points need not be unimodular.

By contrast, at the golden ratio itself (\(t^2 - t - 1\), where \(t + t^{-1} = \sqrt 5 \notin \mathbb{Q}\)) no infinite-order integer point has been found: exhaustively none of length \(\le 12\), and deep beam searches find only the universal \(-I\) torsion.

🚧 Obstructions

  • Determinant. \(\det \rho_\tau(w) = \tau^{\alpha} (\tau - 1)^{2\beta}\) where \(\alpha, \beta\) are the exponent sums of \(\mathsf{A}, \mathsf{B}\) in \(w\). An integral matrix has integer determinant, and if \(\tau\) is a unit this forces a sublattice of allowed \((\alpha, \beta)\). At the golden ratio, \((\varphi - 1)^2 = \varphi^{-2}\) gives \(\det = \varphi^{\alpha - 2\beta}\), so integral words must satisfy \(\alpha = 2\beta\).
  • Trace. \(\operatorname{tr} \rho_\tau(w)\) must be a rational integer — a codimension-\((d-1)\) condition that the beam's off-rational mass measures continuously.
  • Degree. Words built only from \(A^{\pm 1}\) give integer points iff \(\tau^k \in \mathbb{Z}\) (e.g. \(\mathsf{AA} \mapsto \big(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\big)\) at \(\tau = \sqrt 2\)) — recorded but flagged power_of_generator.

🔍 The Beam Search

Exact number-field arithmetic throughout: an element of \(\mathbb{Q}(\tau)\) is a vector of \(d\) rationals in the power basis \(1, \tau, \ldots, \tau^{d-1}\); no floats touch the matrices. Two search modes:

  • fieldSweep.py — exhaustive DFS over all freely reduced words of length \(\le L\) (ground truth; \(4 \cdot 3^{L-1}\) words).
  • integerBeam.py — beam search: grow words breadth-first, keep the best \(W\) matrices at each depth, scored by
    \[ \mathrm{score}(M) = \Big( \log_2 \gcd_{i,j} \mathrm{den}(M_{ij}),\; \textstyle\sum_{k \ge 1} \text{size of coords on } \tau^k,\; \ldots \Big) \]
    where \(\mathrm{den}\) is the denominator of an entry in power-basis coordinates. The gcd-of-denominators score drives the beam toward denominator-free matrices; at unit specializations (denominators identically 1) the off-rational mass — which vanishes exactly on rational matrices — takes over. --score lcm | mixed select variants.

Every integral hit is verified exactly: a \(2\times 2\) integer matrix has finite order iff \(M^{12} = I\) (finite orders in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and \(|\det| \ne 1\) already implies infinite order).
