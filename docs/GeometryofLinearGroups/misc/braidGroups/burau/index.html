🪢 The Braid Group \(B_n\)

The braid group \(B_n\) on \(n\) strands is a fundamental object in topology, algebra, and mathematical physics. It can be thought of as the group of "ways to braid \(n\) strands" up to ambient isotopy.

Presentation

\(B_n\) has generators \(\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_{n-1}\) (the elementary braids) subject to the braid relations:

\[ \sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \quad \text{if } |i - j| \geq 2 \] \[ \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \]

The first relation expresses that distant crossings commute, and the second is the braid (Yang–Baxter) relation involving adjacent generators.

Geometrically, \(\sigma_i\) crosses strand \(i\) over strand \(i{+}1\). The group is infinite and non-abelian. It surjects onto the symmetric group \(S_n\) via the map \(\sigma_i \mapsto (i\ \ i{+}1)\).

Why study representations of \(B_n\)?

  • The Burau representation is faithful for \(n \leq 3\) and known to be unfaithful for \(n \geq 4\).
  • Finding explicit kernel elements — non-trivial braids that act as the identity under Burau — is a computationally interesting challenge.
  • Relations in the Burau image correspond to constraints on the topology of the underlying braids.

📊 The Reduced Burau Representation

The reduced Burau representation is a homomorphism \(\psi: B_n \to \mathrm{GL}_{n-1}\!\big(\mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]\big)\), sending each braid to an invertible matrix over the ring of Laurent polynomials in one variable \(t\).

For \(B_n\) — \((n{-}1) \times (n{-}1)\) Matrices

The representation maps each generator \(\sigma_i\) to an \((n{-}1)\times (n{-}1)\) matrix. The action on the free module with basis \(e_1, \ldots, e_{n-1}\) is:

\[ \sigma_i(e_j) = e_j \quad \text{if } j \neq i{-}1, i, i{+}1 \] \[ \sigma_i(e_{i-1}) = e_{i-1} + t\, e_i, \quad \sigma_i(e_i) = {-t}\, e_i, \quad \sigma_i(e_{i+1}) = e_i + e_{i+1} \]

Faithfulness and the Kernel

A representation is faithful if its kernel is trivial. Burau showed the representation is faithful for \(n \leq 3\), but Moody (1991) proved it is unfaithful for \(n \geq 9\), and Long–Paton and Bigelow pushed this down to \(n \geq 4\). For \(n = 4\), there exist non-trivial braids \(w \in B_4\) with \(\psi(w) = I\).

Finding such elements computationally is the goal of our FlashBeam search.

💍 Quotient Rings

Working in the full Laurent polynomial ring \(\mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]\) leads to coefficient explosion: as words get longer, matrix entries become polynomials of very high degree with huge coefficients.

To make the search tractable, we can specialize to a quotient ring \(\mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}] / (p(t))\), where \(p(t)\) is an irreducible polynomial. This forces \(t\) to satisfy the relation \(p(t) = 0\), bounding the degree of all computations.

Key Specializations
  • \(t = 1\): The Burau representation collapses to the permutation representation. The kernel is the pure braid group.
  • \(t = -1\): Related to homology of double covers. Important in knot theory.
  • \(t = \omega\) (root of unity): The image becomes a finite matrix group, making exhaustive search possible.
  • \(t^2 = 2\): Embeds into \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])\), an arithmetic group of interest.

Relations found in a quotient ring \(R/(p(t))\) correspond to elements of the braid group that act trivially in the specialized representation. These may or may not be in the kernel of the full Burau representation — they are, however, elements of the kernel of the specialized homomorphism.

🌲 Restricting to Subgroups

To focus the search, we can restrict to subgroups generated by subsets of the generators. For instance (when \(n \geq 4\)):

  • \(\langle \sigma_i, \sigma_j \rangle\) with \(|i - j| \geq 2\): These commute, so this is a free abelian subgroup \(\mathbb{Z}^2\). No non-trivial relations expected in any faithful representation.
  • \(\langle \sigma_i, \sigma_{i+1} \rangle \cong B_3\): The Burau rep of \(B_3\) is faithful, so no kernel here.
  • The full group \(B_n\) with all generators: this is where unfaithfulness is known for \(n \geq 4\).

Example: a long relation at \(t = 2\)

The FlashBeam experiments in the local braidGroups repo also study the Birman free subgroup \(F_2 \subset B_4\) generated by \(X = \sigma_3 \sigma_1^{-1}\) and \(Y = \sigma_2 \sigma_3 \sigma_1^{-1} \sigma_2^{-1}\). In the specialized Burau representation at \(t = 2\), the search database records a non-trivial long relation.

Why \(64 = t^6\): the parabolic mechanism

The exponent in the relation above is no accident. Over \(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]\) the reduced Burau matrix of \(u = X^{-1}YXY^{-1}\) is lower triangular with diagonal \((t^4,\, t^{-2},\, t^{-2})\) — and its Levi \(2\times 2\) block is the central matrix \(t^{-2} I\). Consequently, for any word \(p\) whose specialized matrix has first row proportional to \(e_1^{T}\) (i.e. \(p\) lies in the parabolic subgroup \(\widetilde P\) stabilizing the covector flag of \(u\)), the commutator \(v = [p, u]\) automatically lands in the abelian unipotent group \(N = \{\, I + (a e_2 + b e_3)e_1^{T} \,\}\), on which conjugation by \(u^{-1}\) acts as multiplication by \(t^6\) and \(v^k = I + k(v - I)\). At any rational \(t = p/q\) this yields the exact relation

\[ u^{-1}\, v^{\,q^6}\, u \;=\; v^{\,p^6}, \]

and since conjugate powers of an element of a free group must have equal exponents, \(u^{-1} v^{q^6} u\, v^{-p^6}\) is a nontrivial element of \(F_2\) in the kernel of the specialized Burau representation whenever \(v \neq 1\). At \(t = 2\) this explains \(64 = 2^6\). Parabolic words \(p\) can be found by exact projective collisions: whenever two words have projectively equal first rows, \(p = w_1 w_2^{-1} \in \widetilde P\). This produces a shorter unipotent than \(w_2 u^{-1}\) (36 letters instead of 96): with \(p = \texttt{X X X y X X y y X X Y Y X Y X}\) and \(v = [p,u]\), the word \(u^{-1} v u\, v^{-64}\) (2340 letters after free reduction) is verified to be the identity in exact rational arithmetic.

How special is \(t = 2\)?

An exhaustive search (python/rationalKernelSearch.py, python/symbolicParabolicSweep.py; results in data/rational_kernel_db.json) shows the construction is surprisingly rigid:

  • Among all 84 rationals \(p/q\) with \(|p|, q \le 8\) (excluding \(0, \pm 1\)), only \(t = 2\) and its mirror \(t = 1/2\) admit parabolic words built from collision constituents of length \(\le 11\); a deeper scan at \(t = 3\) excludes parabolic words of length \(\le 30\).
  • A symbolic sweep of all \(1{,}062{,}880\) reduced words of length \(\le 12\) over \(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]\) finds no word that is parabolic at any rational \(t \notin \{0, \pm 1\}\) — the shortest at \(t=2\) has length 15. The same sweep finds 145,184 words parabolic at algebraic points, dominated by the cyclotomic factor \(t^2 - t + 1\) (i.e. \(t = \zeta_6\), where \(t^6 = 1\) makes the relation degenerate to commutation), plus genuinely non-cyclotomic specializations such as roots of \(t^2 + t - 1\), \(t^3 - t^2 + 1\), and \(t^4 + t - 1\).
  • The only words of length \(\le 12\) that are parabolic identically in \(t\) are the powers of \(u\) itself. Both length-15 parabolic words at \(t=2\) have first-row gcd exactly \((t-2)(t^2-t+1)\), tying the rational point \(t = 2\) to \(\zeta_6\).
