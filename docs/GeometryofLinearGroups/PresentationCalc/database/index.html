Each pair is listed once up to swapping, inverting either generator, and conjugating by the Klein four-group that fixes the basepoint.

Not Zariski dense — step 1: Γ is finite, virtually abelian, or reducible.

Discrete at a place — step 2: Poincaré proves Γ discrete in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{R}) or \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C}), or the tree algorithm proves it discrete in \mathrm{PGL}_2(K_{\mathfrak{p}}). A presentation is recorded when Poincaré or Bass–Serre reads one off the fundamental domain.

S-arithmetic — step 3: exact coset enumeration in \Lambda = \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathcal{O}_S), Vaserstein’s cusp unipotent theorem, congruence-targeted stabilizer search, or FlashBeam’s tree covering + basepoint stabilizer proves that \Gamma^{(2)} has finite index in \Lambda (up to conjugation).

Thin — step 3: Γ is Zariski dense and not discrete at any single place, yet certified of infinite index in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S]) — either because the vertex orbits on \prod_{p \in S} T_p close up with finite or infinite-index Stallings stabilizers, or by an exact adelic / Bruhat–Tits ping-pong certificate.
