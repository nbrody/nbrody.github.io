What is computed, and how

1 · Zariski closure. The Zariski Closure engine classifies the closure of \Gamma over \overline{\mathbb{Q}}: finite, a torus or its normalizer, a Borel or unipotent group, or all of \mathrm{PGL}_2. A dense \Gamma has an invariant trace field k and quaternion algebra A; its closure over \mathbb{Q} is \mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{PGL}_1(A), and \Gamma^{(2)} lies in an S-arithmetic group \Lambda, where S is the set of primes at which \Gamma is unbounded.

Presentations of groups that are not dense. A finite \Gamma gets the classical presentation of its type. Otherwise \Gamma fixes a point or a pair of points of \mathbb{P}^1. With a fixed point, it acts by z \mapsto \lambda z + b: the multipliers form D \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \times \mathbb{Z}/m (exact multiplicative relations: valuations, and units checked exactly), and the translations form M = \Gamma \cap U, a module over \mathbb{Z}[D], which over \mathbb{Q} is \mathbb{Z}[1/N]. So \Gamma gets a \mathbb{Z}[1/N]-presentation, such as \langle u, t \mid t u t^{-1} = u^{3/2} \rangle for \mathbb{Z}[\tfrac16] \rtimes \mathbb{Z}, in which u^c is the c-th power in M. Bieri–Strebel decides whether it is finitely presented, and with one multiplier an ordinary presentation is given as well (BS(1, n), or BS(b, a) with the commutators that make M abelian). Each new generator is written as a word in g_1, g_2, \ldots, and every relation is checked on the matrices.

Presentations of S-arithmetic groups. Over \mathbb{Q}, the coset table of \Gamma in \Lambda = \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S]) also gives the quotient graph of groups of \Gamma on the tree of the last prime p of S (Bass–Serre). Its vertex groups are the integral subgroups \Gamma \cap h\,\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S \setminus p])\,h^{-1}, presented by Reidemeister–Schreier, or decomposed again along the next prime. To these the graph adds a stable letter for each edge off a spanning tree and an element inverting each inverted edge. The presentation on these generators is shown at the top, and each generator is written as a word in the given ones; the given ones in turn come out of the Bass–Serre normal form.

Congruence closure. The closure \Delta of \Gamma in the congruence topology. The engine finds, prime by prime, where the closure can be smaller than the maximal compact subgroup. Over \mathbb{Q} the local images are then put together in one finite quotient \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/N)/\langle -1, S\rangle, with the sign of the determinant and the parities of its valuations at S. That gives [\Lambda : \Delta] for \Lambda = \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S]). By the congruence subgroup property, \Gamma is S-arithmetic exactly when \Gamma = \Delta, so [\Lambda : \Gamma] \ge [\Lambda : \Delta], with equality exactly for S-arithmetic \Gamma.

2 · Discreteness. For a dense \Gamma, the Discreteness Algorithm tests every place of the field of definition: Poincaré’s polyhedron theorem at \infty and the Bruhat–Tits tree at each prime where \Gamma is unbounded. Embeddings with the same values of \operatorname{tr}^2/\det give conjugate or complex-conjugate groups, so they count as one place, named \mathbb{R}_i or \mathbb{C}_i by its kind; the roots in the field’s picker turn green or red by the verdict at their place. A discrete place is shown live, with its presentation when Poincaré finds one.

3 · Arithmetic, when \Gamma is discrete. \Gamma^{(2)} lies in the arithmetic group \Lambda, a lattice in the product of the places where \Gamma is unbounded. With two or more such places, \Lambda is irreducible and dense at each one, so a \Gamma discrete at one of them is thin. With one, \Gamma is arithmetic exactly when it is a lattice there: Stallings folding in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) or coset enumeration in a Bianchi group decides when the generators lie there, and otherwise Poincaré’s area or volume does; at a prime, the covering certificate below.

3 · Arithmetic, when \Gamma is not discrete at any place. Over \mathbb{Q}, coset enumeration in a presentation of \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S]) runs first, and settles most S-arithmetic groups at once. Over a totally real, CM or cubic field, when \Gamma is bounded at every prime, coset enumeration runs in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathcal{O}_k), presented from Voronoi’s perfect forms; with two or more places at \infty it also checks the stabilizer of o below. When the index is 1, the enumeration is repeated with a word in \Gamma’s generators on every entry of the table (modified Todd–Coxeter), which writes the generators of \Lambda in terms of those of \Gamma. Otherwise FlashBeam searches the adelic product. As in the \mathrm{SO}_3 decision project, \Gamma acts on the product X of the symmetric spaces of the places where it is unbounded (\mathbb{H}^2 or \mathbb{H}^3 at \infty, the tree T_\mathfrak{p} at each \mathfrak{p} \in S) with the \ell^2 metric, and is discrete there. FlashBeam multiplies a beam of words by a persistent flash of the lowest elements found, scored by the height d(o, g\cdot o). Two words reaching one element give a relation, checked exactly. An element g is a Dirichlet generator when the midpoint of [o, g\cdot o] has only o and g\cdot o as nearest orbit points found.

The proof that \Gamma is S-arithmetic comes from the same search: a finite set R of vertices of the trees, with every neighbour of every r \in R equal to w\cdot r' for a word w and some r' \in R, re-checked exactly. Then \Gamma R is every vertex. If A is ramified at every place at \infty this gives finite index; if it splits at one place \sigma, the elements fixing o must also have finite covolume at \sigma, which Poincaré checks. A search that runs out claims nothing.

Orbits without a covering. The covering is often not needed. Suppose \Gamma \subset \mathrm{PGL}_2(k), with k its invariant trace field, and some element of infinite order fixes o. Then \Gamma is not discrete in G_S = \prod_{\mathfrak{p} \in S} \mathrm{PGL}_2(k_\mathfrak{p}). Near the identity, its closure H there is a product of p-adic Lie groups. Each has an \mathrm{Ad}(\Gamma)-invariant Lie algebra, which by Zariski density in \mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{PGL}_2 is a sum of factors \mathfrak{sl}_2(k_\mathfrak{p}). A missing factor would make \Gamma discrete, so H is open. Being unbounded at every \mathfrak{p}, it contains \prod \mathrm{PSL}_2(k_\mathfrak{p}), and \Gamma has the vertex orbits of H: the vertex types that its determinants allow. What remains is the stabilizer of o. The covering search also spreads what it reaches along the elements found fixing each representative.

Cusps. When \Gamma has a parabolic element, a cusp certificate can replace the covering. It applies when the field has infinitely many units or S \ne \varnothing. At the parabolic's fixed point c, the elements of \Gamma fixing c are collected, both among short words and from collisions g(c) = u^s h(c) of the orbit of c modulo the translations u already found (then h^{-1}u^{-s}g fixes c). If their multipliers generate the field and cover S, Vaserstein's theorem for \mathcal{O}_S applies. If the translations alone span a lattice of full rank in the field, and the field has infinitely many units, \Gamma contains a finite-index subgroup of \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}). Its closure at each prime of S is then open and unbounded, so it contains \mathrm{PSL}_2, and \Gamma is S-arithmetic.

Over \mathbb{Q} the vertex orbits on the trees are then enumerated, seeded with FlashBeam’s covering, with each vertex stabilizer kept exactly as a Stallings graph in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}). When they close up, they give the exact index, or show that \Gamma is thin.
