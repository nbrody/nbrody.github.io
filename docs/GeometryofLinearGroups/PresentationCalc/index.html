Presentation Calculator

Enter matrices generating a subgroup \Gamma \le \mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}). The calculator finds its Zariski closure; for a dense \Gamma, whether it is discrete at some place, its congruence closure, and a FlashBeam search for its Dirichlet generators and an S-arithmetic certificate.

What is computed, and how

1 · Zariski closure. The Zariski Closure engine classifies the closure of \Gamma over \overline{\mathbb{Q}}: finite, a torus or its normalizer, a Borel or unipotent group, or all of \mathrm{PGL}_2. A dense \Gamma has an invariant trace field k and quaternion algebra A; its closure over \mathbb{Q} is \mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{PGL}_1(A), and \Gamma^{(2)} lies in an S-arithmetic group \Lambda, where S is the set of primes at which \Gamma is unbounded.

2 · Discreteness. For a dense \Gamma, the Discreteness Algorithm tests every place of the field of definition: Poincaré’s polyhedron theorem at ∞ and the Bruhat–Tits tree at each prime. A discrete place ends the computation and is shown live, with its presentation when Poincaré finds one.

3 · Congruence closure. The closure of \Gamma in \prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\mathrm{PGL}_1(A_\mathfrak{p}), prime by prime. Its index is a lower bound for [\Lambda : \Gamma^{(2)}].

4 · FlashBeam in the adelic product. As in the SO₃ decision project, \Gamma acts on the product X of the symmetric spaces of the places where it is unbounded (\mathbb{H}^2 or \mathbb{H}^3 at ∞, the tree T_\mathfrak{p} at each \mathfrak{p} \in S) with the \ell^2 metric, and is discrete there. FlashBeam multiplies a beam of words by a persistent flash of the lowest elements found, scored by the height d(o, g\cdot o). Two words reaching one element give a relation, checked exactly. An element g is a Dirichlet generator when the midpoint of [o, g\cdot o] has only o and g\cdot o as nearest orbit points found.

The proof that \Gamma is S-arithmetic comes from the same search: a finite set R of vertices of the trees, with every neighbour of every r \in R equal to w\cdot r' for a word w and some r' \in R, re-checked exactly. Then \Gamma R is every vertex. If A is ramified at every place at ∞ this gives finite index; if it splits at one place \sigma, the elements fixing o must also have finite covolume at \sigma, which Poincaré checks. A search that runs out claims nothing.
