What is computed, and how

1 · Zariski closure. The Zariski Closure engine classifies the closure of \Gamma over \overline{\mathbb{Q}}: finite, a torus or its normalizer, a Borel or unipotent group, or all of \mathrm{PGL}_2. A dense \Gamma has an invariant trace field k and quaternion algebra A; its closure over \mathbb{Q} is \mathrm{Res}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{PGL}_1(A), and \Gamma^{(2)} lies in an S-arithmetic group \Lambda, where S is the set of primes at which \Gamma is unbounded.

Congruence closure. The closure \Delta of \Gamma in the congruence topology. The engine finds, prime by prime, where the closure can be smaller than the maximal compact subgroup. Over \mathbb{Q} the local images are then put together in one finite quotient \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/N)/\langle -1, S\rangle, with the sign of the determinant and the parities of its valuations at S. That gives [\Lambda : \Delta] for \Lambda = \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S]). By the congruence subgroup property, \Gamma is S-arithmetic exactly when \Gamma = \Delta, so [\Lambda : \Gamma] \ge [\Lambda : \Delta], with equality exactly for S-arithmetic \Gamma.

2 · Discreteness. For a dense \Gamma, the Discreteness Algorithm tests every place of the field of definition: Poincaré’s polyhedron theorem at ∞ and the Bruhat–Tits tree at each prime where \Gamma is unbounded. Embeddings with the same values of \operatorname{tr}^2/\det give conjugate or complex-conjugate groups, so they count as one place, named \mathbb{R}_i or \mathbb{C}_i by its kind; the roots in the field’s picker turn green or red by the verdict at their place. A discrete place is shown live, with its presentation when Poincaré finds one.

3 · Arithmetic, when Γ is discrete. \Gamma^{(2)} lies in the arithmetic group \Lambda, a lattice in the product of the places where \Gamma is unbounded. With two or more such places, \Lambda is irreducible and dense at each one, so a \Gamma discrete at one of them is thin. With one, \Gamma is arithmetic exactly when it is a lattice there: Stallings folding in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) or coset enumeration in a Bianchi group decides when the generators lie there, and otherwise Poincaré’s area or volume does; at a prime, the covering certificate below.

3 · Arithmetic, when Γ is not discrete at any place. Over \mathbb{Q}, coset enumeration in a presentation of \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/S]) runs first, and settles most S-arithmetic groups at once. Otherwise FlashBeam searches the adelic product. As in the SO₃ decision project, \Gamma acts on the product X of the symmetric spaces of the places where it is unbounded (\mathbb{H}^2 or \mathbb{H}^3 at ∞, the tree T_\mathfrak{p} at each \mathfrak{p} \in S) with the \ell^2 metric, and is discrete there. FlashBeam multiplies a beam of words by a persistent flash of the lowest elements found, scored by the height d(o, g\cdot o). Two words reaching one element give a relation, checked exactly. An element g is a Dirichlet generator when the midpoint of [o, g\cdot o] has only o and g\cdot o as nearest orbit points found.

The proof that \Gamma is S-arithmetic comes from the same search: a finite set R of vertices of the trees, with every neighbour of every r \in R equal to w\cdot r' for a word w and some r' \in R, re-checked exactly. Then \Gamma R is every vertex. If A is ramified at every place at ∞ this gives finite index; if it splits at one place \sigma, the elements fixing o must also have finite covolume at \sigma, which Poincaré checks. A search that runs out claims nothing.

Over \mathbb{Q} the vertex orbits on the trees are then enumerated, seeded with FlashBeam’s covering, with each vertex stabilizer kept exactly as a Stallings graph in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Z}). When they close up, they give the exact index, or show that \Gamma is thin.
