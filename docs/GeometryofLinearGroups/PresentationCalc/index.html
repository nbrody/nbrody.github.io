How it Works

1 · Zariski closure

The Zariski Closure engine classifies the closure of Γ over ℚ̄: finite, a torus or its normalizer, a Borel or unipotent group, or all of PGL₂. A Zariski-dense Γ has an invariant trace field k and quaternion algebra A, its closure over ℚ is Resk/ℚ PGL₁(A), and Γ⁽²⁾ lies in an S-arithmetic group Λ of it, where S is the set of primes at which Γ is unbounded.

2 · Discreteness

For a dense Γ, the Discreteness Algorithm tests every place of the field of definition: Poincaré’s polyhedron theorem at ∞ and the Bruhat–Tits tree at each prime. A discrete place ends the search and shows Γ there, with its presentation when Poincaré finds one.

3 · Congruence closure

The closure of Γ in ∏𝔭∉S PGL₁(A𝔭), prime by prime (Dickson’s classification and Serre’s lifting lemma away from finitely many primes; exact images mod 𝔭m at the rest). Its index is a lower bound for [Λ : Γ⁽²⁾].

4 · FlashBeam in the adelic product

As in the SO₃ decision project, Γ is studied in the product X of the symmetric spaces of every place where it is unbounded: H² or H³ at ∞, the tree T𝔭 at each 𝔭 ∈ S, with the ℓ² metric. Γ is discrete there. FlashBeam multiplies a beam of words by a persistent flash of the lowest elements found, scored by the height d(o, g·o). Two words reaching one element give a relation, checked exactly. An element g is a Dirichlet generator when the midpoint of [o, g·o] has only o and g·o as nearest orbit points. Its bisector then carries a facet of the Dirichlet domain. These are measured against the points found, as in the SO₃ project.

The proof that Γ is S-arithmetic comes from the same search. It needs a finite set R of vertices of the trees, with o ∈ R, and for each neighbour n of a vertex of R, a word w and r ∈ R with w·r = n. Pairs of words whose images are adjacent count too. Then Γ·R is every vertex, because the product of trees is connected. Every word is checked again exactly.

If A is ramified at every place at ∞, vertex stabilizers in Λ are finite, and Γ has finite index in Λ. If A splits at one place σ at ∞, the elements of Γ fixing o that the search found must also have finite covolume at σ. Poincaré checks this from a Dirichlet domain (relations exact, geometry in floating point, as in Poincaré itself). Then Γ is S-arithmetic. A search that runs out claims nothing.

Modular

The input, exact arithmetic and domains are Poincaré’s; the Zariski and congruence data are the Zariski Closure engine’s; the places are the Discreteness Algorithm’s; the trees are Trees’. Changes to those tools appear here.
