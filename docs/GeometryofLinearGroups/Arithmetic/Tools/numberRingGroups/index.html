    How it works

    Forms. Let $V$ be the real vector space of Hermitian forms on $(k\otimes\R)^2$, one $2\times 2$ Hermitian matrix $A_\sigma$ for each place $\sigma$ of $k$ at infinity, with the trace pairing $\langle A, B\rangle = \sum_\sigma c_\sigma \tr(A_\sigma B_\sigma)$ ($c_\sigma = 2$ at complex places, $1$ at real ones). For $v\in\O_k^2$ put $q(v) = vv^*$, so that $\langle A, q(v)\rangle = A[v]$. A positive definite form $P$ is perfect if it is determined by its minimum and the set $\Min P$ of vectors attaining it. Its Voronoi cone is

    $$\sigma(P) = \cone\{\, q(v) : v \in \Min P \,\}.$$

    Koecher's theorem: these cones and their faces tile the cone $C$ of positive definite forms (together with its rational boundary), $\GL_2(\O_k)$ permutes them with finitely many orbits, and the cones meeting the interior have finite stabilizers. The interior modulo scaling, $C^\circ/\R_{>0} \cong (\H^2)^{r_1}\times(\H^3)^{r_2}\times\R^{r_1+r_2-1}$, is contractible.

    Voronoi's algorithm. Start from the trace form and move it, keeping its minimal vectors, until they span $V$: a perfect form. For each facet of $\sigma(P)$ with inner normal $D$, the neighbor is $P + tD$ for the largest $t$ that keeps the minimum. Two perfect forms are equivalent when some $g\in\GL_2(\O_k)$ carries one set of minimal vectors onto the other, found by matching a pair of minimal vectors. The stabilizer of a cone is the set of such $g$ from the form to itself. The convex geometry (minimal vectors, facets, faces of codimension two) is exact. For totally real fields the coordinates are symmetric matrices over $k$, for CM fields Hermitian matrices over $k$, and for cubic fields with a complex place all coordinates of $q(v)$ lie in the real cubic field $\tau(k)$, since $|\sigma(x)|^2 = N(x)/\tau(x)$.

    Brown's theorem. Pick one cell $C_i$ per orbit, with stabilizer $S_i$, and for each $S_i$-orbit of facets $F$ an element $g_F$ with $g_F C_j$ the cell across $F$. Then $\GL_2(\O_k)$ is generated by the $S_i$ and the $g_F$, with relations

    • $g_F = 1$ when $C_j$ was first reached across $F$ (a spanning tree of the cell orbits);
    • a presentation of each finite group $S_i$, certified by coset enumeration;
    • $h\, g_F = g_F\, h'$ for $h$ in the stabilizer of $F$ in $S_i$, with $h' = g_F^{-1} h g_F \in S_j$;
    • $g_F\, t\, g_{F'}\, r = 1$, crossing $F$ and coming back ($t\in S_j$, $r\in S_i$);
    • for each orbit of faces of codimension two meeting $C^\circ$, the product of the crossings around it lies in $S_i$.

    The other groups. The center of $\GL_2(\O_k)$ is $\O_k^\times = \mu_k\times\langle\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_{r-1}\rangle$, computed by the same algorithm in rank one (Voronoi's algorithm for units). Adding $\zeta I = \varepsilon_j I = 1$ presents $\PGL_2(\O_k) = \GL_2(\O_k)/\O_k^\times$. The words for $\varepsilon_j I$ come from walking along a segment from a cell to its image, as in section 4. $\PSL_2(\O_k)$ is the kernel of the determinant $\PGL_2(\O_k)\to\O_k^\times/\O_k^{\times 2}$ and $\SL_2(\O_k)$ that of $\GL_2(\O_k)/\langle\varepsilon_j I\rangle\to\O_k^\times/\langle\varepsilon_j^2\rangle$, both by Reidemeister–Schreier. Tietze moves shorten the results, and every relator is checked as a matrix identity in exact arithmetic.

    Scope. Totally real fields, CM fields, and cubic fields with one complex place. Other fields with complex places would need coordinates in the Galois closure. The number of perfect forms grows quickly with the degree and the discriminant. Here $\PGL_2(\O_k)$ means $\GL_2(\O_k)/\O_k^\times$. When the class group has $2$-torsion, the normalizer of $M_2(\O_k)$ in $\PGL_2(k)$ is larger; it is not computed here.
