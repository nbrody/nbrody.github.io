The group and its character variety

$\Gamma = \langle a, b \mid [a,b]^2 \rangle$ is the orbifold fundamental group of a torus with a single cone point of angle $\pi$: killing the square of the commutator turns the boundary of the once-punctured torus into a rotation of order 2. Its orbifold Euler characteristic is $-\tfrac12$, so it carries hyperbolic structures.

For $\rho:\Gamma \to \SL_2$, the traces $x = \tr\rho(a)$, $y = \tr\rho(b)$, $z = \tr\rho(ab)$ determine the character, and every word's trace is an integer polynomial in $(x,y,z)$. The Fricke identity gives $$\tr\,\rho([a,b]) \;=\; x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xyz - 2 \;=\; \kappa(x,y,z) - 2 .$$

Why κ = 2 is the surface

In $\SL_2$ itself, $[A,B]^2 = I$ forces $[A,B] = \pm I$. The case $[A,B] = I$ gives the reducible characters, the nodal Cayley cubic $\kappa = 4$; the case $[A,B] = -I$ is achieved only by the quaternion representation $a, b \mapsto i, j$, the single character $(0,0,0)$ on $\kappa = 0$.

The geometric representations live in $\PSL_2$: there $\rho([a,b])$ is the rotation by $\pi$ about the cone point, whose lifts have trace $0$. So the character variety of the orbifold is the smooth cubic surface $$X:\quad x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xyz = 2 .$$ Its real locus is a compact "pillow" of $\SU(2)$-characters together with four unbounded sheets; the Teichmüller space of the orbifold sits inside a sheet, with the most symmetric (hexagonal) structure at the irrational point $\big(1{+}\sqrt3\big)(1,1,1)$ — no star shines exactly there.

Stars, heights, and gold

$X$ is a cubic surface with rational points, hence unirational over $\Q$: its rational points are Zariski-dense. Each star is a rational point, drawn with radius $H^{-\alpha}$ where $H$ is the height — the largest numerator or denominator among the three coordinates in lowest terms. Unusual cancellation makes low, bright stars, in the spirit of the algebraic number starscapes of Harriss–Stange–Trettel (arXiv:2008.07655). Integral points are drawn gold, warm stars lie on the compact $\SU(2)$ part, icy ones on the unbounded sheets.

On the Markov cone $\kappa = 0$ the origin is a cone singularity, and projecting from it enumerates every rational point exactly once: a primitive triple $(u,v,w)$ gives $$\left( \tfrac{u^2+v^2+w^2}{vw},\; \tfrac{u^2+v^2+w^2}{uw},\; \tfrac{u^2+v^2+w^2}{uv} \right).$$ The integral points are precisely $3\times$(Markov triples) — the gold stars — and the quaternion character glows at the origin. Integral points on the whole family $\kappa = k$ are the "Markoff-type surfaces" studied by Ghosh–Sarnak.

Knot and link groups

For a two-bridge knot group $\langle a, b \mid aw = wb\rangle$ the generators are conjugate meridians, so every character has $x = y = t$ and the variety is a curve in the plane $x = y$: the abelian characters trace the parabola $z = t^2 - 2$, and the nonabelian characters form their own curve, meeting the parabola exactly where $t^2 - 2$ hits a root of the Alexander polynomial.

Trefoil $\langle a,b \mid aba = bab\rangle$: the nonabelian curve is the line $z = 1$ — rational points are dense beads (they meet the parabola at $t^2 = 3$, irrational, so the two strands of stars never collide).

Figure-eight knot: elimination gives the nonabelian curve $$z^2 - (t^2+1)\,z + 2t^2 - 1 = 0,$$ which meets the parabola at $t^2 = 5$ (the Alexander roots of $u^2-3u+1$) and at the cusp $t = 2$ recovers $z = \tfrac{5 \pm i\sqrt3}{2}$, the trace field $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$. A rational point needs $(t^2-1)(t^2-5)$ to be a rational square — a genus-one condition, so by Mordell–Weil the rational stars are scarce: a search through denominator $120$ finds only $t = \pm 1$, the two gems $(\pm1,\pm1,1)$ where the $\SU(2)$ arc meets the $\mathrm{SL}(2,\R)$ branches. Real nonabelian characters exist for $|t| \le 1$ ($\SU(2)$) and $|t| \ge \sqrt5$ (Fuchsian).

Whitehead link: the meridians of the two components are independent, so the nonabelian characters form a genuine surface, $$z^3 - xyz^2 + (x^2+y^2-2)\,z - xy = 0,$$ symmetric in $x \leftrightarrow y$ and containing the coordinate lines $(t,0,0)$, $(0,t,0)$. At the cusp $x = y = 2$ it gives $z = 1 \pm i$ — the trace field $\Q(i)$. Its $z$-fibers are conics, so the same chord-parametrization and quadratic-flip machinery applies; rational points are plentiful. (The abelian characters of the link form the Cayley cubic — the κ = 4 entry above.)

Symmetries and the Markov tree

Twisting $\rho$ by the four characters $\Gamma \to \{\pm 1\}$ flips signs of coordinates in pairs, and the mapping class group acts by permutations together with the Vieta involutions such as $z \mapsto xy - z$ (each coordinate is a root of a quadratic whose other root is again rational). How the points are found · controls

For $\kappa = 0$, the cone projection above is a complete search. Otherwise the tool sweeps all $(x,y)$ with bounded denominator and tests the discriminant $x^2y^2 - 4x^2 - 4y^2 + 4k$ for being a rational square, then walks the conic fibers $\{x = \xi\}$ through every known point by chord parametrization, and closes up under Vieta moves.
