Four Groups over ℚ

Each group is $A^\times/\mathbb{Q}^\times$ for an algebra $A$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The four main columns come in pairs: a split form and an anisotropic form of the same group over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

The tori $\mathbb{Q}^\times \subset \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$ and $\mathrm{SO}_2(\mathbb{Q}) \subset \mathrm{SO}_3(\mathbb{Q})$ sit inside the bigger groups as the diagonal torus and the rotations about an axis, matching $\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q} \subset M_2(\mathbb{Q})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(i) \subset \mathbb{H}$. Two optional torus columns add a second imaginary quadratic field, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, and a real one, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$.

Notes

The two Euler–Rodrigues formulas

Both are the action of $A^\times$ on the trace-zero part of $A$ by conjugation, written in coordinates. Entries are quadratic in the four coordinates and the denominator is the norm.

Split: $g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$

\mathrm{Ad}(g) = \frac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{pmatrix} ad+bc & -ac & bd\\ -2ab & a^2 & -b^2\\ 2cd & -c^2 & d^2\end{pmatrix}

Basis $H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $E=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $F=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, preserving $-\det(xH+yE+zF) = x^2+yz$. Setting $b=c=0$ gives $\operatorname{diag}(1,\,a/d,\,d/a)$: the $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ column.

Anisotropic: $q=a+bi+cj+dk\in \mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q})^\times$

R_q = \frac{1}{N(q)}\begin{pmatrix} a^2+b^2-c^2-d^2 & 2(bc-ad) & 2(bd+ac)\\ 2(bc+ad) & a^2-b^2+c^2-d^2 & 2(cd-ab)\\ 2(bd-ac) & 2(cd+ab) & a^2-b^2-c^2+d^2\end{pmatrix}

Acting on pure quaternions $xi+yj+zk$, preserving $x^2+y^2+z^2$. Setting $c=d=0$ gives the rotation about the $x$-axis by $(a+bi)^2/(a^2+b^2)$: the $\mathrm{SO}_2(\mathbb{Q})$ column.

Denominators come from the norm

For a primitive integral element $g$ of $A$, the reduced denominator of its image in $G(\mathbb{Q})$ is $|N(g)|$ with the primes where $A$ ramifies removed: 2 for $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb{H}$, 3 for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, none for the split algebras. So the element over a ramified prime ($1+i$, $\sqrt{-3}$, $\sqrt2$) gives an integral matrix. That is why $G(\mathbb{Z})$ is bigger than the image of the ring units in those columns.

Checked by exhaustive computation: every primitive input with entries in [−6, 6] for the 4-variable formulas, and in [−30, 30] for the quadratic fields.

Hilbert 90, uniformly

1 \to \mathbb{Q}^\times \to A^\times \to G(\mathbb{Q}) \to H^1(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{G}_m)=0

So $G(\mathbb{Q}) = A^\times/\mathbb{Q}^\times$ in every column, not just $G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$. The norm-one group $A^1$ is the spin group. Its image is the kernel of the spinor norm $\theta = N \bmod \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}$, and the cokernel is $N(A^\times)/\mathbb{Q}^{\times2}$.

Lines and trees

Primitive integral elements of norm $p^k$, up to units, are the vertices at distance $k$ from a base vertex. In the quaternion columns, at a prime where $G$ is noncompact, there are $(p+1)p^{k-1}$ of them: the $(p+1)$-regular tree. In the torus columns there are 2, the two ends of a line. That line is an apartment of the tree, and the torus is its stabilizer.

The optional tori

$\mathrm{SO}(x^2+xy+y^2)$ is the hexagonal version of $\mathrm{SO}_2$. Its rational points give integer triangles with a $120^\circ$ angle, such as 3-5-7. The torsion is $\mathbb{Z}/6$ and the split primes are $p\equiv1 \bmod 3$.

$\mathrm{SO}(x^2-2y^2)$ is anisotropic over $\mathbb{Q}$ but noncompact over $\mathbb{R}$. So $G(\mathbb{Z})=\pm(3+2\sqrt2)^{\mathbb{Z}}$ is infinite with compact quotient: Pell's equation, i.e. Dirichlet's unit theorem. In the quaternion family the same position is held by $B^\times/\mathbb{Q}^\times$ for an indefinite division algebra, such as $B=(-1,3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ of discriminant 6, whose unit groups are cocompact Fuchsian groups.

Pythagorean triples in the $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ column

In the model $a^2-b^2=1$, the point for $t=m/n$ is $a=\frac{m^2+n^2}{2mn}$, $b=\frac{m^2-n^2}{2mn}$. This is the same triple $(m^2+n^2,\ m^2-n^2,\ 2mn)$ as for $\mathrm{SO}_2$, with $2mn$ as the denominator instead of $m^2+n^2$.

Columns: $\mathbb{Q}^\times = \mathrm{SO}(xy)(\mathbb{Q})$, $\mathrm{SO}_2(\mathbb{Q}) = \mathrm{SO}(x^2+y^2)(\mathbb{Q})$, $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}) = \mathrm{SO}(x^2+yz)(\mathbb{Q})$, $\mathrm{SO}_3(\mathbb{Q}) = \mathrm{SO}(x^2+y^2+z^2)(\mathbb{Q})$. Math is typeset by KaTeX as MathML.
