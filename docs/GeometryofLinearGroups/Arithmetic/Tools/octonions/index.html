1 · The quaternion picture

For an odd prime $p$ the Hurwitz order satisfies $\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb Z_p\cong M_2(\mathbb Z_p)$. A primitive $\alpha\in\mathbb H$ of norm $p^k$ determines the right ideal $\alpha\mathbb H$, a vertex at distance $k$ from the base vertex of the Bruhat–Tits tree $T_p$, and a factorization $\alpha=\pi_1\pi_2\cdots\pi_k$ with $N(\pi_i)=p$ is the geodesic $$\mathbb H\supset\pi_1\mathbb H\supset\pi_1\pi_2\mathbb H\supset\cdots\supset\alpha\mathbb H .$$ It is unique up to unit migration ($24^{k-1}$ choices). With several primes, each ordering of the multiset of primes is a monotone path in $\prod_p T_p$, and $\mathrm{P}\mathbb H^\times(\mathbb Z[1/N])\cong \mathrm{SO}_3(\mathbb Z[1/N])=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb H[1/N])$ acts on $\prod_{p\mid N}T_p$. Bracketing never matters.

2 · Octavian factorization (Conway–Smith, Rehm)

$\mathbb O$ is Coxeter's maximal order in the octonions, an $E_8$ lattice for $N(x)=x\bar x$. There are $240(p^3+1)$ octavians of norm $p$, for every prime $p$ (including $2$: $\mathbb O\otimes\mathbb Q_p$ is split at every prime).

Theorem. Let $\alpha$ be primitive with $N(\alpha)=p_1p_2\cdots p_k$, the primes in any order, and fix any bracketing $T$. Then $\alpha$ has exactly $240^{k-1}$ factorizations $\alpha=[\pi_1\pi_2\cdots\pi_k]_T$ with $N(\pi_i)=p_i$.

The engine rests on one lemma (Rehm): for $m\mid N(\alpha)$, the left divisors of $\alpha$ of norm $m$ are exactly the $240$ minimal vectors of the lattice $\alpha\mathbb O+m\mathbb O\cong\sqrt m\,E_8$ (right divisors: $\mathbb O\alpha+m\mathbb O$). So each node of the bracketing tree is a shortest-vector problem in an $E_8$, solved here by exact LLL and enumeration. At each node the calculator chooses the right factor primary: $\gamma\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb O}$ and first nonzero coordinate positive, which is unique when $N(\gamma)$ is odd. Then every factor after the first is primary up to sign and the unit sits in the first factor. For the left comb this is Rehm's unique factorization $\alpha=(\cdots((\varepsilon\pi_1)\pi_2)\cdots)\pi_k$ with $\pi_i$ primary. The other $239$ choices at a node are its metamigrations. Signs are central, so they are collected in the first factor.

3 · What does not survive

  • Divisibility is not transitive. In $((\pi_1\pi_2)\pi_3)$, $\pi_1$ divides $\pi_1\pi_2$, which divides $\alpha$, yet typically $\pi_1\nmid\alpha$ (about $93\%$ of random triples, $3\cdot5\cdot7$).
  • No action on lattices. $x(y\mathbb O)\neq(xy)\mathbb O$ (in every test), and $x\mapsto\pi x\pi^{-1}$ is never an automorphism for a prime $\pi$, so $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\mathbb O\alpha^{-1}$ produces no orders.
  • The unit loop has no orbits. The sets $\mathbb O^\times\pi$ overlap. Only for Rehm's primary primes $P(p)$ do they partition the primes of norm $p$. Mod $p$, the $p^3+1$ solids $\mathbb O\pi/p\mathbb O$ ($\pi\in P(p)$) form a spread of the hyperbolic quadric $Q_7^+(p)$, the octonion stand-in for the $p+1$ points of $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)$.

So the projective octavians are a Moufang loop, not a group, and factorizations are not the paths of a group acting on a building. (An octonionic LPS construction, Dahan–Tillich arXiv:1011.2642, was withdrawn after a lemma of exactly this kind failed.)

4 · What does survive: the box

Left multiplication $L_\alpha$ is a similitude of the norm form, so the natural home of octavian arithmetic is $\mathrm{Spin}_8$ with triality, and its buildings of type $\tilde D_4$. For primitive $\alpha$ the lattices $$B_d(\alpha)=\alpha\mathbb O+d\mathbb O\qquad(d\mid N(\alpha))$$ are vertices of $\prod_p\mathcal B(\mathrm{Spin}_8(\mathbb Q_p))$. At each $p$ they lie on the geodesic segment from $\mathbb O$ to $\alpha\mathbb O$ (elementary divisors $(1^4,(p^k)^4)$: a spin-coweight direction), so together they form a product of segments, the box of $\alpha$. The two-factor splits $\alpha=\beta\gamma$ with $N(\beta)=d$ are exactly the minimal vectors of $B_d(\alpha)$, so an ordering of the primes is a staircase path through the box, exactly as for quaternions.

A full factorization is a tree of splits. Only the root split is guaranteed to sit on the box. A subproduct on the left spine of the tree (a product $\pi_1\cdots\pi_j$, however bracketed inside) is a left divisor of $\alpha$, i.e. lies in $B_{d_j}(\alpha)$, only when divisibility happens to compose. The Box & paths tab draws the box, the staircase of the current ordering, and marks every spine subproduct as on or off the box. For quaternions (ring ℍ) every mark is on.

One identity does transport boxes: Moufang's $(u\alpha)(\mathbb Ou)=u(\alpha\mathbb O)u$. At a prime $q\nmid N(u)$ this says $(u\alpha)\mathbb O_q=B_u(\alpha\mathbb O_q)$ with $B_u(x)=uxu$, one component of the triality triple $(B_u,L_u,R_u)$, not $L_u$.

5 · G₂ = Aut(𝕆) and its buildings

The analogue of $\mathrm{SO}_3=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb H)$ is $G_2=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb O)$. $\mathrm{Aut}$ of the octavians is $G_2(\mathbb F_2)$, of order $12096$, and the genus has class number one (Gross, Groups over ℤ). So $G_2(\mathbb Z[1/N])=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb O[1/N])$ acts transitively on the hyperspecial vertices of $\prod_{p\mid N}\mathcal B(G_2(\mathbb Q_p))$, with stabilizer $G_2(\mathbb F_2)$. This is the true analogue of the quaternion statement, for the group.

  • Apartments: the $30$–$60$–$90$ tiling. Hyperspecial vertices (type 0) form a triangular lattice, type 1 vertices are edge midpoints (4 chambers, $\mathrm{SO}_4$-type), type 2 vertices are triangle centres (6 chambers, $\mathrm{SL}_3$-type). Each panel lies in $p+1$ chambers.
  • The link of a hyperspecial vertex is the split Cayley hexagon $H(p)$: points are isotropic points of $\mathrm{Im}(\mathbb O/p\mathbb O)$, lines are planes $\langle x,y\rangle$ with $xy=0$. This tab builds it from the octavians mod $p$.
  • Hyperspecial vertices at position $\lambda=a\,\omega_s+b\,\omega_\ell$ number $p^{\langle2\rho,\lambda\rangle}W(p^{-1})/W_\lambda(p^{-1})$, with $\langle 2\rho,\omega_s\rangle=6$ and $\langle2\rho,\omega_\ell\rangle=10$. In particular there are $p+p^2+\cdots+p^6$ nearest ones and $p^5+\cdots+p^{10}$ at the long step.
  • Factorization in $G_2(\mathbb Z[1/N])$. If $g\mathbb O$ sits at position $(a_p,b_p)$ at each $p$, then for each ordering of the $\sum(a_p+b_p)$ steps there is a unique chain of hyperspecial vertices, because the spherical Hecke algebra has $c^{\mu+\nu}_{\mu\nu}=1$. By transitivity, $g=h_1\cdots h_m\,u$ with each $h_i$ a single short or long step at one prime ("$G_2$-primes") and $u\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb O)$, unique up to $G_2(\mathbb F_2)$-migration. There are two kinds of prime at each $p$, and there is no bracketing because this is a group.
  • How the G₂ tab computes it, exactly. An element is given by $g(e_1),g(e_2),g(e_3)$, a basic triple of rational unit octonions. Its position at $p$ is read from the $p$-adic elementary divisors of $g\mathbb O$ relative to $\mathbb O$, which are $\pm(a+2b),\pm(a+b),\pm b,0,0$. The vertex one step from $\mathbb O$ toward $T=r\mathbb O$ is a basis-free lattice expression: $$\text{short: }(p^{-1}\mathbb O\cap p^{b}T)+(\mathbb O\cap p^{-b}T)+p\mathbb O,$$ $$\text{long: }(p^{-2}\mathbb O\cap p^{a+b-1}T)+(p^{-1}\mathbb O\cap T)+(\mathbb O\cap p^{1-b}T)+(p\mathbb O\cap p^{1-a-b}T)+p^{2}\mathbb O .$$ (In a common apartment $T$ has exponents $c$ and the step vertex has exponents $f(c)$ for an odd step function $f$, which is a minimum of maxima of the hinges $\max(x,\,y+c)$, i.e. a sum of intersections $p^x\mathbb O\cap p^yT$.) The prime $h$ is then the first isomorphism $\mathbb O\to R$ found among basic triples of the $126$ imaginary units of the maximal order $R$, in a fixed order. That makes the factorization canonical, and the product is re-multiplied as a check. The tests confirm: every step vertex is a maximal order at the predicted position, $|\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbb O|=126\cdot96=12096$, $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb O)$ moves one short neighbour at $2$ onto exactly $2+4+\cdots+64=126$ maximal orders, and the maximal order at each vertex of the box is the same for every ordering.
  • Where octavians meet $G_2$. $G_2(\mathbb F_p)$ has two orbits on the points of $Q_7^+(p)$. One consists of the $(p^6-1)/(p-1)$ trace-zero points, which form the link $H(p)$. The other consists of the $p^3(p^3+1)$ idempotent points, each with stabilizer $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb F_p)$. A primitive $\alpha$ with $p\,\|\,N(\alpha)$ lands in the hexagon iff $p\mid\mathrm{tr}\,\alpha$. For $p>4$ a prime of norm $p$ does so only if it is pure imaginary.

6 · The Fano plane and E₈

The products of $e_1,\dots,e_7$ are encoded by the Fano plane with lines $\{a,a+1,a+3\}$: $e_ae_{a+1}=e_{a+3}$ cyclically, and reversing two factors changes the sign. Each line spans a quaternion subalgebra $\langle1,e_a,e_b,e_c\rangle$. Its intersection with $\mathbb O$ is a Hurwitz order exactly when $\{0,a,b,c\}$ is a codeword of the Hamming code defining $\mathbb O$. That holds for $\{1,2,4\},\{2,3,5\},\{2,6,7\}$; for the other four lines the intersection is only the Lipschitz order. The $240$ units of $\mathbb O$ are the roots of $E_8$. Among them, the $126$ imaginary units form $E_7$, and each Hurwitz order contributes a $D_4$ (a $24$-cell). The Fano & E₈ tab draws them in the Coxeter plane of $E_8$: $8$ rings of $30$, with neighbours at $60°$ joined.
