Unravelling $\PGL_2(\Z[1/p])$

The group $\Gamma = \PGL_2(\Z[1/p])$ is dense in $\PGL_2(\R)$, so the hyperbolic plane alone sees nothing discrete. Add the $p$-adic place and it acts on $\mathbb{H}^2 \times T_{p+1}$. There the modular group $\Lambda = \PGL_2(\Z)$ is the stabiliser of a vertex and $t = \begin{psmallmatrix} p&0\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}$ steps to the next one. Reading the tree as a graph of groups gives an HNN extension, folded once. Walking the tree gives a normal form for every element.

1

A tree of Farey tessellations

$\Gamma$ acts on the tree $T_{p+1} = \PGL_2(\Q_p)/\PGL_2(\Z_p)$ through its $p$-adic embedding. In the upper-half-plane model the vertices are

$$\fl{q}{n} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} p^n & q \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot o, \qquad n \in \Z,\quad q \in \Z[1/p]/p^n\Z,$$

with $o = \fl{0}{0}$ and $q$ taken in $[0, p^n)$. The parent of $\fl{q}{n}$ is $\fl{q}{n-1}$ (towards $\infty$). Its $p$ children are $\fl{q + jp^n}{n+1}$ for $0 \le j < p$. The stabiliser of $o$ is $\Gamma \cap \PGL_2(\Z_p) = \PGL_2(\Z) = \Lambda$. Since the upper triangular matrices above lie in $\Gamma$, the action is transitive, so vertices are cosets $\Gamma/\Lambda$.

The same coset acts on $\mathbb{H}^2$. $\Lambda$ is the full symmetry group of the Farey tessellation $\mathcal{F}$, so the vertex $\fl{q}{n}$ carries the tessellation $p^n\mathcal{F} + q$, which is well defined because $\mathcal{F} + 1 = \mathcal{F}$. One tree, one tessellation per vertex: that is the picture of $\mathbb{H}^2 \times T_{p+1}$ to keep in mind. Click a vertex to see its tessellation.

2

Two subgroups of index $p+1$, and one conjugation

$\Lambda$ permutes the $p+1$ edges at $o$ through its action on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Two edges matter: the edge up to $t^{-1}o = \fl{0}{-1}$ and the edge down to $t\,o = \fl{0}{1}$. Their stabilisers in $\Lambda$ are the two congruence subgroups

$$\begin{aligned} \htmlClass{c-up}{\Lambda_0(p)} &= \Lambda \cap t^{-1}\Lambda t = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\c&d \end{pmatrix} \in \Lambda : c \equiv 0 \bmod p \right\} = \mathrm{Stab}_\Lambda\bigl(t^{-1}o\bigr), \\ \htmlClass{c-down}{\Lambda^0(p)} &= \Lambda \cap t\Lambda t^{-1} = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\c&d \end{pmatrix} \in \Lambda : b \equiv 0 \bmod p \right\} = \mathrm{Stab}_\Lambda\bigl(t\,o\bigr), \end{aligned}$$

each of index $p+1$. Since $t$ carries the first edge onto the second, it conjugates the first stabiliser onto the second:

$$t\begin{pmatrix} a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}t^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} a & pb \\ c/p & d\end{pmatrix}, \qquad t\,\htmlClass{c-up}{\Lambda_0(p)}\,t^{-1} = \htmlClass{c-down}{\Lambda^0(p)}.$$

Subscript means the lower-left entry is divisible by $p$, as for $\Gamma_0(N)$. The thesis (§2.3) writes $\Lambda_0(p)$ for the upper-right version, i.e. for $\Lambda^0(p)$ here.

3

An HNN extension, folded once

One vertex orbit, vertex stabiliser $\Lambda$, and an element $t$ moving $o$ to a neighbour. Bass–Serre theory then points to the HNN extension in which $t$ identifies the two finite-index subgroups:

$$\Lambda *_t \;=\; \bigl\langle\, \Lambda,\ t \;\bigm|\; t g t^{-1} = \varphi(g) \text{ for } g \in \Lambda_0(p) \,\bigr\rangle, \qquad \varphi(g) = tgt^{-1} \in \Lambda^0(p).$$

Including $\Lambda$ and sending $t \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(p,1)$ defines a surjection $\Lambda*_t \to \Gamma$. It is not injective. In $\PGL_2$ the flip $\sigma \in \Lambda$ satisfies

$$\sigma\, t\, \sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 1&0\\0&p \end{pmatrix} = t^{-1}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad W_p = \sigma t = \begin{pmatrix} 0&1\\p&0\end{pmatrix},\quad W_p^2 = 1.$$

The Fricke involution $W_p$ swaps $o$ and $t^{-1}o$, so $\Gamma$ acts on $T_{p+1}$ with an inversion. In $\Lambda *_t$, by contrast, $\sigma t$ has infinite order.

Folding. The Bass–Serre tree of $\Lambda*_t$ is $(2p+2)$-regular. At $o$ there are $p+1$ outgoing edges $c\,t\cdot o$, one for each $c$ in the transversal $\{T^0,\dots,T^{p-1},\sigma\}$ of $\Lambda/\Lambda^0(p)$. There are also $p+1$ incoming edges $c'\,t^{-1}\cdot o$, one for each $c'$ in the transversal $\{1, T^0\sigma,\dots,T^{p-1}\sigma\}$ of $\Lambda/\Lambda_0(p)$. The relation glues them in pairs: $\sigma t\cdot o = t^{-1}\cdot o$ and $T^j t\cdot o = T^j\sigma t^{-1}\cdot o$. The result is $T_{p+1}$.

Theorem

$\Gamma \;=\; \Lambda *_t \,\big/\, \langle\!\langle (\sigma t)^2 \rangle\!\rangle \;\cong\; \Lambda \ast_{\Lambda_0(p)} \langle \Lambda_0(p), W_p\rangle.$ The quotient graph of groups is a half-edge. The vertex group is $\Lambda$ and the edge group is $\Lambda_0(p)$. At the midpoint of $[t^{-1}o, o]$ the group is the Fricke extension $\langle\Lambda_0(p), W_p\rangle$, which contains $\Lambda_0(p)$ with index 2.

Why not an honest HNN extension?

Every HNN extension maps onto $\Z$: send the stable letter to 1 and the base group to 0. $\Gamma$ has no such map. In its abelianisation, $t = \sigma\cdot W_p$ is a product of two involutions, so $2[t] = 0$. Together with the finite group $\Lambda^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong (\Z/2)^2$ this makes $\Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}$ a finite 2-group. So $\Gamma$ splits over $\Lambda_0(p)$ only as the amalgam above, and the fold cannot be avoided. (The same happens for $\mathrm{SL}_2$: $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z[1/p]) = \mathrm{SL}_2(\Z) \ast_{\Gamma_0(p)} \mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$, with two vertex orbits instead of an inversion.)

Nothing is lost for normal forms. The fold glues one up-edge to one down-edge at each vertex, so Britton's normal form survives once we agree which of the two glued labels to use: $t^{-1}$ for the edge toward $\infty$, and $T^j t$ for the $p$ edges away from it. This is exactly the parent/child structure of the upper-half-plane model.

4

The normal form, and how to compute it

Normal form

Every $g \in \PGL_2(\Z[1/p])$ has a unique expression

$$g \;=\; \htmlClass{c-up}{t^{-m}}\cdot \htmlClass{c-down}{(T^{j_1}t)(T^{j_2}t)\cdots(T^{j_k}t)}\cdot \htmlClass{c-lam}{\lambda}, \qquad m,k\ge 0,\quad 0\le j_i \lt p,\quad \lambda\in\PGL_2(\Z),$$

with $j_1 \neq 0$ whenever $m,k>0$. The prefix is the coset representative of Prop. 17:

$$t^{-m}(T^{j_1}t)\cdots(T^{j_k}t) = \begin{pmatrix} p^n & q\\ 0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad n = k-m,\quad q = p^{-m}\bigl(j_1 + j_2 p + \cdots + j_k p^{k-1}\bigr).$$

The word traces the geodesic from $o$ to $g\cdot o$: $m$ steps up toward $\infty$, then $k$ steps down, reading the base-$p$ digits of $q$. Its length is $m + k = d(o, g\cdot o) = v_p(\det\tilde g)$, where $\tilde g$ is the primitive integral representative of $g$.

Uniqueness. For any word of this shape, the vertices $o,\ w_1 o,\ w_1w_2 o,\dots$ form a path without backtracking, since the condition $j_1\ne0$ is exactly what rules out turning back at the top. A path without backtracking in a tree is the geodesic $[o, g\cdot o]$, which pins down every letter. Then $\lambda$ is (prefix)$^{-1}g$, which fixes $o$. Existence is the algorithm.

Algorithm (peel one edge at a time)
  1. Replace $g$ by its primitive integral representative, with $\det g = \pm p^e$.
  2. While $e > 0$, reduce $g$ mod $p$. It has rank one. Its column space is a point of $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p) = \Lambda/\Lambda^0(p)$, i.e. an edge at $o$: the first edge of the geodesic $[o, g\cdot o]$.
    • $[1:0]$, i.e. $\row_2 \equiv 0$: the edge goes up. Emit $\htmlClass{c-up}{t^{-1}}$ and divide $\row_2$ by $p$. In HNN terms the coset is $\sigma\Lambda^0(p)$, so $g = \sigma t\cdot g'$, folded by $\sigma t = t^{-1}\sigma$.
    • $[j:1]$, i.e. $\row_1 \equiv j\,\row_2$: the edge goes down through digit $j$. Emit $\htmlClass{c-down}{T^j t}$ and replace $\row_1$ by $(\row_1 - j\,\row_2)/p$. The coset is $T^j\Lambda^0(p)$, so $g = T^j t\cdot g'$.
    Each step lowers $e$ by exactly one, since we move one edge closer along the geodesic.
  3. What is left has $\det = \pm 1$: it is $\htmlClass{c-lam}{\lambda} \in \PGL_2(\Z) = \mathrm{Stab}(o)$. The Euclidean algorithm writes it in $S, T, R$.
