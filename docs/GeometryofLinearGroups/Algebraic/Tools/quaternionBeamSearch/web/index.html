Quaternion beam search

Pick two integer quaternions $a,b$ whose reduced norms are prime powers $p^{x}$ and $q^{y}$. The tool runs a Reidemeister–Schreier beam search on the Bruhat–Tits tree $T_{q}$ to extract the elements of $\langle a,b\rangle$ that lie in $\mathrm{PH}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])$, factors them by the Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak rule into the free group $F_{(p+1)/2}=\langle\text{norm-}p\text{ quaternions}\rangle$, and folds them into a Stallings graph to certify finite index.

How it works

    
    
      
  • Trees. $5\equiv13\equiv1\ (4)$ and more generally any odd $q$ split
      the Hamilton algebra: $H\otimes\mathbb{Q}_q\cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_q)$, so
      $\langle a,b\rangle$ acts on the $(q{+}1)$-regular tree $T_q$.
    • 
      
  • Roles. $a$ (norm a power of $p$) is a unit at $q$, so it is
      elliptic — it fixes the base vertex $v_0$. $b$ (norm a power of $q$) is
      hyperbolic — it translates $v_0$.
    • 
      
  • The intersection.
      $\langle a,b\rangle\cap\mathrm{PH}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])=\mathrm{Stab}(v_0)$: the
      words whose primitive norm is a pure power of $p$. We find them by
      Reidemeister–Schreier — every fold in the orbit BFS is a generator that
      fixes $v_0$.
    • 
      
  • The “hint”. $a$ acts on the link of $v_0$ as an element of
      $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ of some order $m$; $a^{m}$ fixes the whole
      radius-1 ball, so $b\,a^{m}b^{-1}$ lands back in $\mathrm{PH}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])$.
      The search discovers these folds automatically.
    • 
      
  • Certificate. Each generator is turned into a reduced word in
      $F_{(p+1)/2}$ by LPS unique factorisation and folded into a Stallings graph.
      Finite index $\iff$ the graph is complete (every vertex carries all
      generators); the index is the vertex count.
