Zariski Closure

Enter matrices generating a subgroup \Gamma \le \mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}). See its Zariski closure and, when \Gamma is Zariski dense, the trace field, quaternion algebra and arithmetic group that contain it.

Examples

Generators

2×2 matrices as ((a,b),(c,d)) or [[a,b],[c,d]], separated by commas. Entries can use i, sqrt(…), cbrt(…), zeta_n and ω.

What is computed, and how

Each radical is adjoined to the field of entries K at its principal complex value, so relations such as \sqrt{-2} = i\sqrt2 are respected. Matrices are taken up to scalars, so \operatorname{tr}^2/\det is the basic invariant.

Over \overline{\mathbb{Q}} the closure is decided exactly. Reducibility comes from the span of \Gamma. Element orders come from Kronecker's theorem. An invariant pair of points comes from a regular semisimple element. The finite primitive groups A_4, S_4, A_5 are found by enumeration.

For dense \Gamma, the invariant trace field k = \mathbb{Q}(\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Ad}\Gamma) is read off the \mathbb{Q}-span of \operatorname{Ad}\Gamma. The quaternion algebra is A = (\operatorname{tr}^2 G - 4,\ \operatorname{tr}[G,H] - 2)_k for G = g^2/\det g and H = h^2/\det h. Its ramification is computed from tame symbols, a finite dyadic search and the real embeddings. S is the set of primes where \operatorname{tr}^2/\det fails to be integral on words of length at most 3; these are exactly the places where \Gamma is unbounded.

\Gamma is unbounded at a real place where A splits, at every complex place, and at the primes of S, and bounded everywhere else. The symmetric space X is the product of \mathbb{H}^2, \mathbb{H}^3 and Bruhat–Tits trees over those places.

Congruence closure. At a prime \mathfrak{p} \notin S the closure of \Gamma is compact, so \Gamma fixes a vertex of the tree, possibly not the standard one. The page first lists a finite set of primes: those dividing 2\cdot 3\cdot 5, the discriminant of k, the ramification of A, a commutator trace \operatorname{tr}[G,H]-2, the values of \operatorname{tr}^2/\det that A₄, S₄, A₅ allow, and the index of the trace ring. Every other prime is full, by Dickson and Serre. At the listed primes the image of \Gamma modulo \mathfrak{p}^m is computed at a fixed vertex (a \Gamma-stable lattice, or a maximal order containing the order generated by \Gamma). Kernels come from Schreier's lemma, and p-th powers or commutators show when deeper levels are full. The closure is reported prime by prime. The closure in the product equals the product of these local closures unless two local quotients are correlated, which can only happen through small coincidences such as \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_4) \cong \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_5).

Zariski density does not imply finite index: the page shows the arithmetic group containing \Gamma and its congruence closure, not whether \Gamma is all of it.
