The Tree of Planes

SL₂(ℤ[t]) acting on the Bruhat–Tits tree of ℚ((1/t)), with a hyperbolic plane at every vertex.

The picture

Put $\pi = 1/t$, so $\mathbb{Z}[t] \subset K = \mathbb{Q}((\pi))$, whose valuation ring $\mathbb{Q}[[\pi]]$ has residue field $\mathbb{Q}$. The link of each vertex of the tree of $\mathrm{SL}_2(K)$ is $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$, the rational cusps of a hyperbolic plane, so every vertex $v$ gets a plane $X(v)$ and every cusp of $X(v)$ carries the plane of a neighbour.

The base vertex $v_0$ is the lattice $\mathbb{Q}[[\pi]]^2$; its stabilizer is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, acting on $X(v_0)$ as usual. The plane at $p/q$ hangs off the cusp $p/q$ as its reflected Ford circle, joined by an edge of length $1/q$.

On the half-plane the tree climbs toward its end $\infty$: the planes at $\infty$, then at the cusp $0$ of that one, and so on, are the ray from $v_0$ to the end (the lattices $\langle e_1, \pi^k e_2\rangle$), and each sits above its parent where every other plane hangs below. With equal edges a plane's height is then its horocycle level about $\infty$, the Busemann function that goes up one along each edge toward $\infty$ and down one along every other.

The colours follow a rule chosen in the View tab: the type of the vertex (the tree is bipartite), its distance from $v_0$, its horocycle level, the branch at $v_0$ it lies in, the height of its cusp, or how far the current element moves it. They belong to the place a plane sits, or, with Colours travel with the planes, to the vertex whose picture it shows, so that the action visibly moves them.

Frames and the action

A vertex is named by its path of cusps $w_1, \dots, w_n$ from $v_0$, and its plane is drawn in the frame $g_v = T_{w_1} \cdots T_{w_n}$ with $T_w = \begin{pmatrix} \pi & w \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $T_\infty = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ \pi & 0 \end{pmatrix}$: the cusp $\infty$ of every plane points back to its parent.

An element $\gamma$ sends $v$ to $\gamma v$, and $g_{\gamma v}^{-1}\gamma\, g_v$ is, up to a power of $\pi$, in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Q}[[\pi]])$; its reduction mod $\pi$ is the Möbius map $X(v) \to X(\gamma v)$. The animation moves every plane to its image while turning its contents by that map. All of it is computed exactly.

When the base plane itself moves, the upper half-plane rolls up into a disk, through the round disks $z \mapsto z/(1 - i\tau z/2)$ with $\tau$ going from $0$ to $2$ (each one a conformal picture of $\mathbb{H}$), and the plane taking its place unrolls the same way.

$\gamma$ translates an axis of the tree by $\max(0, v(\det\gamma) - 2v(\operatorname{tr}\gamma))$, which is $2\max(0, \deg \operatorname{tr}\gamma)$ in $\mathrm{SL}_2$; otherwise it fixes a vertex, or swaps the ends of an edge when $v(\det\gamma)$ is odd.

Over ℚ(i) and ℚ(ω)

For $K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ or $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$, $\omega = \tfrac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}$, the residue field of $K((1/t))$ sits in $\mathbb{C}$, so every vertex carries hyperbolic 3-space $\mathbb{H}^3$, drawn as a ball, and its link $\mathbb{P}^1(K)$ is the set of $K$-rational points of its sphere at infinity. The child at $p/q$, with $p, q \in \mathcal{O}_K$ coprime, is the reflected Ford sphere there, of diameter $2/(1+|p|^2+|q|^2)$, on an edge of length $1/|q|$; the slider bounds the height $|p|^2+|q|^2$.

The circles on each sphere pass through neighbouring cusps, those with $ps - qr$ a unit: they are the images $g(\hat{\mathbb{R}})$ for $g \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, and the Ford spheres of neighbours touch.

$\mathrm{PGL}_2(K(t))$ acts by the same exact computation as over $\mathbb{Q}$, with coefficients in $K$: the local map of $\gamma$ at a ball is the reduction of $g_{\gamma v}^{-1}\gamma\, g_v$, now in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(K) \subset \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$, which moves the ball by an orientation-preserving isometry of $\mathbb{H}^3$ and its circles by the Möbius map on the sphere. In the animation every ball moves along the one-parameter subgroup through its local map — a rotation, a parabolic slide or a screw motion — with its children riding the sphere.

Specializing t

Evaluating at $t = a \neq 0$ sends $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}[t, 1/t])$ to $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, which acts on a single plane $\mathbb{H}$. The frames specialize too, and $z \mapsto g_v(a)\cdot z$ identifies every plane $X(v)$ with $\mathbb{H}$; the collapse lays each plane onto the base that way, so the plane carried at $u = gv$ lands as $g(a)\,g_v(a)$. At $t = 1$ the planes at integer cusps land exactly on the base tessellation; at $t = 2$ the plane at $0$ lands scaled by $\tfrac12$.

The tree is the other end of the same family: $g_{\gamma v}(T)^{-1}\,\gamma(T)\,g_v(T)$ tends to the local map as $T \to \infty$. So the collapse does not commute with the tree's action, and the collapsed plane carries its own: $\gamma$ acts there by $\gamma(a)$, rigidly.

All of PGL₂(ℚ(t))

The whole of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}((\pi)))$ acts, so every matrix over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ does: entries are expanded in powers of $\pi = 1/t$, and since scaling changes nothing, each generator is cleared of denominators first. The local maps then lie in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, which acts on $\mathbb{H}$ by isometries; those of negative determinant reverse orientation, and the animation turns such a plane over about its diameter through $\infty$ — in three dimensions a reflection is a half-turn.

An element whose determinant has odd valuation, like $\operatorname{diag}(t, 1)$, swaps the two colour classes of the tree; the planes change colour as they move.

Using it

Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom; click a plane to fly to it, and hover one to see its path from $v_0$. H hides the panel.
