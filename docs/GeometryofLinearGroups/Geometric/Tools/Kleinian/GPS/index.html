The interbreeding construction

Gromov and Piatetski-Shapiro (1988) built the first non-arithmetic lattices in O(n,1) for every n by interbreeding: take two arithmetic hyperbolic manifolds M₁, M₂ that contain isometric embedded totally geodesic hypersurfaces, cut both along these hypersurfaces, and glue M₁⁺ to M₂⁻ across the cut. If Γ₁ and Γ₂ are non-commensurable, the resulting hybrid is a lattice that belongs to no arithmetic commensurability class.

What this tool computes (nothing is hard-coded)

Working in ℍ² with the forms f = s₁x² + c·y² − s₃z² on ℤ³:

  • Vinberg's algorithm finds a fundamental polygon for the maximal reflection subgroup of O(f, ℤ): roots e with f(e) = k > 0 and k | 2s₁e₁, 2c·e₂, 2s₃e₃ are accepted outward from the base point p₀ = (0,0,1) while all pairwise angles stay ≤ π/2, until the chamber has finite area. Some forms are non-reflective; then the tool says so instead of drawing something false.
  • Because f₁ and f₂ restrict to the same binary form g = s₁x² − s₃z² on {y = 0}, both groups contain the reflection in the geodesic Σ = {y = 0}, and each tiling is cut cleanly by Σ. The picture shows the Γ₁-tiling on one side and the Γ₂-tiling on the other — the universal cover of the hybrid piece by piece.
  • Commensurability is decided exactly. A ternary form is classified up to similarity by its even Clifford quaternion algebra C₀(f) = (−s₁c, s₁s₃ / ℚ), and Γ₁, Γ₂ are widely commensurable iff C₀(f₁) ≅ C₀(f₂). The tool computes the ramification sets from Hilbert symbols. Empty ramification ⇔ f isotropic over ℚ ⇔ the piece has cusps.

Reading the verdicts

If the ramification sets differ, the pieces are non-commensurable and the GPS theorem makes the hybrid non-arithmetic — this is the mechanism that works in every dimension. If they agree, the pieces are commensurable and GPS is silent (the hybrid can even be arithmetic). A quirk of ℚ-forms in dimension 2: any two cusped pieces are commensurable (both algebras are M₂(ℚ)), so a genuine GPS verdict here needs at least one cocompact piece.

Gluing caveat, honestly stated

The wall patterns that Γ₁ and Γ₂ induce on Σ need not match point-for-point, and the pictures don't pretend they do. Both stabilizers of Σ are commensurable arithmetic subgroups of Isom(Σ) determined by g (a Pell equation, here), so after passing to suitable finite covers the boundary circles become isometric and the gluing is by an isometry — that is where GPS's interbreeding actually happens. The triangle-group mode instead constrains both pieces to induce the same pattern on Σ (a π/p corner and a cusp), so there the drawn orbifolds glue directly.

In dimension 2 non-arithmetic lattices are plentiful (polygon moduli); the point of GPS — and of the quaternion-algebra criterion computed here — is that the same cut-and-glue argument works rigidly in dimensions ≥ 3, where nothing else did. Reference: M. Gromov, I. Piatetski-Shapiro, Non-arithmetic groups in Lobachevsky spaces, Publ. Math. IHÉS 66 (1988).
