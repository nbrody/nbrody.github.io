    What the colours are

    Γρ = ⟨A, B⟩ has two cusps, ∞ (fixed by A) and 0 (fixed by B). The involution E(z) = 1/(ρz) swaps them and conjugates A to B, so Γ̂ = ⟨A, E⟩ contains Γρ with index 2. The Ford domain of Γ̂ at ∞ is the set of points in the slab |Re z| ≤ ½ that are at least as close to the horoball at ∞ as to every horoball of either cusp orbit, with horoballs at ∞ and 0 swapped by E. Its faces are the isometric spheres of the elements g ∈ Γρ (solid) and of gE (hatched). A Ford domain of Γρ computed at ∞ alone only sees the solid ones. Switch to “Cusp ∞ only” to see that picture: its first wall is the Lyndon–Ullman circle |ρ − 2| = 2, not Riley's |ρ| = 4.

    Riley's own palette (pick “Riley (original)”) indexes the face count, mod A-translation: 1 black, 3 blue, 5 green, 7 red, 9 aquamarine, 11 magenta, 13 azure, 15 yellow, then it repeats. Recomputed this way, the picture matches Riley's original pixel for pixel, up to the pleating rays drawn over it.

    The walls

    |ρ| = 4 (black | blue). The isometric circle of E is |z| = |ρ|−½. For |ρ| ≥ 4 it misses its translates, and E is the only face. On the wall it touches its translate at ½. Inside, the faces of B and B⁻¹ = EA±1E are born at E(∓½) = ∓2/ρ.

    |ρ| = 2(1 + cos arg ρ) (blue | green), a cardioid through the cusps 2i and 4. On it, the circles of E, EA⁻¹ and B⁻¹ pass through the single point ½(1 − i tan(arg ρ / 2)). Inside, the new faces B⁻¹AE and BA⁻¹E are born at that point's images under B∓1.

    Every wall works this way. A vertex of the Ford domain reaches the sphere at infinity: there, three or more isometric circles are concurrent. Crossing the wall, Poincaré's cycle condition at the new vertex forces new faces. Approaching a cusp ρp/q of the slice, the p/q Farey word turns parabolic and the face count goes to infinity. That is why the walls pile up into scallops at every cusp.

    Faces are computed exactly: power-diagram cells by half-plane clipping, words grown by products f A±1 g of faces until nothing new is visible. Code: fordCore.js. The static picture: node rileyRegions.js.
