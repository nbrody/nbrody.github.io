The Riley Slice

The $\rho$ for which $\Gamma_\rho$ is discrete and free. Full explanation · Geometric Tools

The picture

The region the rays surround is (approximately) where $\Gamma_\rho = \langle X, Y \rangle$, with $X = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $Y = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\\rho&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, fails to be free and discrete; its complement is the Riley slice.

The colored curves are the Keen–Series pleating rays: for each slope $p/q$, the branch of $\Phi_{p/q}^{-1}\big((-\infty, -2]\big)$ asymptotic to $\arg\rho = \pi p/q$, traced inward from $\infty$ by Newton continuation. They are pairwise disjoint and land on the $p/q$ cusp. The dots are the roots of the Farey polynomials $\Phi_{p/q} + 2$.

Extensions continue each ray past its cusp into the interior along $\Phi_{p/q} \in (-2, 2]$, where the $p/q$ word is elliptic. The beads mark $\Phi_{p/q} = -2\cos(\pi/n)$: the Heckoid groups of slope $p/q$ and order $n$, accumulating at the cusp as $n \to \infty$.

The dots are computed for every slope with $q \le 128$, about 400 000 of them, by Aberth iteration in background workers, with $\Phi_{p/q}$ evaluated by its Stern–Brocot recursion (never from its expanded coefficients, which reach $10^{25}$). The picture shows the lowest slopes up to a fixed number of dots on screen, so their density stays the same as you zoom, and sizes them by $q$ relative to the deepest slope shown. Every root converges, even in the tight clusters at $\pm 4$: $P/P'$ is scaled before it is formed, steps are capped so no iterate reaches where $\Phi$ overflows, and a root stops only when its own Newton step is negligible.

Ford regions at both cusps

Riley's original picture. The color counts the faces of the Ford domain of $\langle X, E \rangle$, $E(z) = 1/(\rho z)$, which swaps the cusps $\infty$ and $0$ and conjugates $X$ to $Y$: the Ford domain of $\Gamma_\rho$ taken at both of its cusps (Riley's palette: 1 black, 3 blue, 5 green, 7 red, …).

It is computed exactly (power cells by half-plane clipping, faces closed under products) in parallel workers, once per region: along each row the walls come from the Ford domain's algebraic certificate, and nearly every wall is a corner of the ideal boundary being covered by a third circle.

The first walls are $|\rho| = 4$ (white), where the isometric circle of $E$ meets its translate, and the cardioid $|\rho| = 2(1 + |\cos\arg\rho|)$ (black), where the circles of $E$, $EX^{-1}$ and $Y^{-1}$ are concurrent; dashed, the one-cusp wall $|\rho \mp 2| = 2$. In the $\sqrt\rho$ plane they become the circle $|z| = 2$, the four circles $|z \mp 1| = 1$, $|z \mp i| = 1$, and two lemniscates. Each later wall is a vertex of the Ford domain reaching the sphere at infinity, and the walls pile up at every cusp.

Ford regions at $\infty$ alone

The Ford domain of $\Gamma_\rho$ at $\infty$, computed algebraically. Its faces over the $p/q$ arc are powers $W^k$ of the Farey word, visible if and only if $|S_{k-1}(\Phi_{p/q})| < |S_{j-1}(\Phi_{p/q})| + |S_{k-j-1}(\Phi_{p/q})|$ for all $0 < j < k$ ($S_m$ the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind), so each fan opens on $|\Phi_{p/q}(\rho)| = 2$.

Its edges record which visible isometric circles overlap, $|C - C' - n| < r + r'$: crossing an edge wall relinks the face graph without changing the faces, carving each fan into the curvilinear panels of Jørgensen's picture. Color = face pairs, shade = linking class. The first wall is the Lyndon–Ullman circle $|\rho - 2| = 2$, not Riley's $|\rho| = 4$: it misses the faces of the cusp at $0$.

The $\sqrt\rho$ plane

The $z$ for which $\left\langle \left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&z\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right), \left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\z&1\end{smallmatrix}\right) \right\rangle$ is discrete and free. Conjugating by $\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt z, 1/\sqrt z)$ turns this pair into the Riley generators with $\rho = z^2$, so the picture is the pullback of the Riley slice under $z \mapsto z^2$: a double cover, symmetric under $z \mapsto -z$, with cusps at $\pm 2$ and $\pm 2i$.

The preview

It follows the cursor like the Julia set beside a Mandelbrot set. Limit set: the limit set of $\Gamma_\rho$, which is $X$-periodic, by a depth-first search over reduced words beginning with $Y^{\pm 1}$. A branch stops when the images of three of its limit points lie within a pixel, and its color records the first two letters ($Y\cdots$ indigo, $Y^{-1}\cdots$ teal).

Polyhedron: the Ford domain of the chosen group, ray-cast in upper half-space. The hemispheres are its faces (those of the cusp at $0$ lighter), the glass walls $\operatorname{Re} z = \pm\tfrac12$ close it into one period of $X$, and the bare floor around them is its free side, where the domain of discontinuity shows. Both pictures first look for a Shimizu witness.

Exact values

A click on a dot (a root of $\Phi_{p/q} + 2$) or a bead (a root of $\Phi_{p/q} + 2\cos(\pi/n)$) factors its polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$, keeps the irreducible factor through that root, and polishes the root with Newton steps evaluated exactly. PresentationCalc then runs on that exact group, and its verdicts (Zariski closure, discreteness at every place, arithmeticity) appear under the selection. “Open exactly in Dirichlet tool” passes the factor along as a root row, so the domain there is computed in the number field.
