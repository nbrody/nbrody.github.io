About the Riley Slice
The Riley slice is a 2-dimensional cross-section of Schottky space. It parametrizes two-generator Kleinian groups where one generator is parabolic. The different colored regions represent combinatorially distinct types of Dirichlet domains.
📖 Read Full Explanation
What the Colors Mean
The gray region is the certified complement of the Riley slice, with its fractal boundary: gray points are within a small Newton distance of a root of a Farey polynomial 2 − Φp/q (root sets are dense in the complement — Elzenaar–Martin–Schillewaert), or in the Shimizu-excluded disk |ρ| < 1, or on the real segment (−4, 4). Raising the Farey depth sharpens the fractal boundary. The exterior is tiled Riley-style: each colored region is labeled by the slope p/q whose Farey word has minimal |Φ| — the dominant word of the Dirichlet/Ford domain, as in Riley's original computer plot of combinatorial types. Coarse slopes own the large outer regions; bands of finer regions accumulate on the boundary. The thin dark curves laminating the exterior are the pleating rays, drawn Yamashita-style from each p/q cusp out to infinity.
Key Features
What do I see here?
Colored Regions: A Riley-style tessellation of the slice: within one region a single Farey word Wp/q has the smallest |Φ|, so the Dirichlet domain keeps the same dominant combinatorics throughout. Walls appear where dominance passes to a Farey-neighbor slope; the regions refine into curvy bands as they approach the boundary.
Gray Region: The complement of the Riley slice, rendered via the root sets of the Farey polynomials 2 − Φp/q: the roots lie in, and are dense in, the complement, so the union of small neighborhoods of roots converges to the true fractal-boundary region as the depth grows. Seeded with the Shimizu disk |ρ| < 1 and the real segment (−4, 4).
Pleating Rays: Curves in the exterior where the Farey trace Φp/q is real and ≤ −2. Each ray runs from the p/q cusp on the boundary out to infinity in the asymptotic direction πp/q; distinct rays never cross.
Ray Extensions: Fainter curves continuing each ray past its cusp into the non-discrete region, where Φp/q is real in (−2, 2) and the word Wp/q becomes elliptic.
Boundary Curves: The curves separating regions, marking where a trace polynomial passes through ±2.

Mathematical Background
Learn more
Each point ρ in the Riley slice corresponds to a group Γ(ρ) with generators:
A = ((1,1),(0,1))
B = ((1,0),(ρ,1))
The combinatorial type is determined by which Farey traces satisfy |Φ_{p/q}(ρ)| < 2. This encodes how many faces meet at the origin in the Dirichlet domain.
