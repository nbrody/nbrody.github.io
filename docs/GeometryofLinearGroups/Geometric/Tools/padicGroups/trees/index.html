Vertices

A vertex is a ball $\lfloor x \rfloor_k = x + \pi^k\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak p}$ in $K_{\mathfrak p}$, where $\pi$ is a uniformizer. Its label is canonical: the $\pi$-adic digits of $x$ below $\pi^k$, with digits drawn from a fixed set of $q$ representatives of the residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ these are the familiar labels $x\in\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ with $0 \le x < p^k$.

Number Fields

Turn on Number Field and give a generator and its minimal polynomial. The prime $p$ factors in $K$ as $p\,\mathcal{O}_K = \prod \mathfrak p_i^{e_i}$ with residue fields $\mathbb{F}_{p^{f_i}}$; choose one $\mathfrak p_i$ to choose the tree. Its link at every vertex is $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_{p^f})$, so it is $(p^f+1)$-regular.

$e = f = 1$. $K_{\mathfrak p} = \mathbb{Q}_p$, and $\mathfrak p$ is an embedding $K \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}_p$. It sends the generator to a $p$-adic root of its minimal polynomial: by Hensel's lemma each simple root mod $p$ lifts uniquely, so these choices are the flexibility in embedding $K$. The labels are the rationals of the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-tree.

$f > 1$. $K_{\mathfrak p}$ contains the unramified extension of degree $f$; the links grow to $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_{p^f})$, and the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-tree is the subtree of vertices with all digits in $\{0,\dots,p-1\}$.

$e > 1$. The uniformizer $\pi$ is not $p$; levels count powers of $\pi$, so the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-tree sits inside with each edge cut into $e$.

Everything is exact: valuations come from an anti-uniformizer of $\mathfrak p$ in a $p$-maximal order (Round 2), with no $p$-adic approximation. Minimal polynomials whose ring $\mathbb{Z}[w]$ is not maximal (e.g. $w^2+3$ at $2$) are handled.

Action Formula

With $v = v_{\mathfrak p}$ and $\lfloor x\rfloor_k = x + \pi^k\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak p}$:

$$ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\cdot \lfloor x \rfloor_k \;=\; \begin{cases} \Big\lfloor \frac{a\,x + b}{c\,x + d} \Big\rfloor_{\,k+v(\det g)-2v(cx+d)} & \text{if }v(cx+d)-v(c)\leq k \\ \Big\lfloor \frac{a}{c} \Big\rfloor_{\,v(\det g)-2v(c)-k} & \text{if }v(cx+d)-v(c)> k \end{cases} $$

Group Action

$\mathsf{PGL}_2(K)$ acts through $K\subset K_{\mathfrak p}$ by the formula in the Orbit tab. A generator $g$ has translation length $\ell(g)=\max\{0,\ v(\det g)-2\,v(\operatorname{tr} g)\}$; it is hyperbolic when this is positive and otherwise fixes a vertex or the midpoint of an edge.

How It Works

Bounded groups. If every generator and every product of two generators is elliptic, the group fixes a point of the tree (Serre's lemma and the Helly property). It is then discrete exactly when finite, which is decided exactly: elements of finite order $m$ in $\mathsf{PGL}_2(K)$ have $\varphi(m)\le 2[K:\mathbb{Q}]$.

Two generators. Conder's Nielsen test on translation lengths (arXiv:1908.11114) certifies a free, discrete, purely hyperbolic pair.

Any rank. Markowitz's reduction (arXiv:2308.16359) applies Nielsen moves that shorten the displacement $|g| = d(v, g v)$, breaking ties by the order of initial half-paths, until the basis is N-reduced — then the group is free on it and discrete, checked independently against N1–N3 — or contains an elliptic element. An elliptic of infinite order lies in a compact stabilizer, so the group is not discrete.

Torsion. A finite-order elliptic does not decide. The group passes to the congruence kernel mod a prime $\mathfrak q$ with $e(\mathfrak q|\ell) < \ell - 1$, which is torsion-free and of finite index; the same reduction then decides it. The complete two-generator classification of Conder–Schillewaert (arXiv:2208.12404) is not implemented wholesale. A resource limit gives unknown, never a verdict.
