Discreteness Algorithm

Generators of a subgroup Γ of PGL₂(ℚ̄). Is there a place of their field at which Γ is discrete?

Entries are read exactly as in Poincaré: fractions and decimals, i, radicals, roots of unity, cos and sin at rational multiples of π, and roots of polynomials. The chosen root only names your embedding; every embedding is tested.

Places

Each place of the field of definition F gives a metric on F, and a topology on PGL₂ of its completion. Click a place to see Γ there.

How it Works

The field of definition

A matrix in PGL₂ is defined only up to a scalar, so each generator is divided by its first nonzero entry. The entries then generate the smallest field F with Γ ⊂ PGL₂(F), written F = ℚ(w) for a primitive element w with minimal polynomial f. A place of a larger field gives the same answer as the place of F below it, so testing the places of F tests every place.

Places at ∞

One for each real root of f, and one for each pair of complex conjugate roots (a conjugate group is a mirror image). At each, Γ is read for that embedding and handed to Poincaré, whose canonical Dirichlet domain and polyhedron theorem prove discreteness. Non-discreteness is proved exactly from short words: an elliptic element of infinite order (an elliptic of order m in PGL₂(F) has φ(m) ≤ 2[F:ℚ]), or a pair breaking Shimizu’s lemma.

Finite places

At a prime 𝔭, PGL₂(F𝔭) acts on its Bruhat–Tits tree. If the normalized entries are 𝔭-integral and the determinants 𝔭-units, Γ fixes the standard vertex: it is bounded, and then discrete exactly when it is finite. This holds for all but the finitely many primes dividing a denominator or the norm of a determinant; one prime outside them decides finiteness for all of them. The rest are decided by Trees: Serre–Helly boundedness, Conder’s two-generator test, Markowitz’s reduction, and congruence kernels for torsion.

What the verdicts mean

Discrete and Not discrete are proofs. Undecided means a search ran out: Poincaré’s conditions failed at this word length, or the tree decision ran out of time. Γ can be discrete at no single place and still be discrete in a product of them, as a subgroup of PGL₂(ℤ[½]) is in PGL₂(ℝ) × PGL₂(ℚ₂): the S-arithmetic picture.

Modular

The input, exact arithmetic and domains are Poincaré’s modules; the local fields and the tree decision are Trees’ modules; each view is the tool itself. Changes to either tool appear here.
