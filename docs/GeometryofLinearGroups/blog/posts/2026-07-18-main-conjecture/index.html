The classification conjecture: classifying subgroups of algebraic groups

July 18, 2026

This site is organized around a single question: given a simple algebraic group $\G$ over a number field $k$, what are the finitely generated subgroups of $\G(k)$? After studying a certain number of examples, one may observe some patterns in how subgroups can be constructed. We give rigorous definitions of these construction schemes, and conjecture that these completely exhaust the possible ways to construct subgroups of $\G(k)$.

Three types of subgroups

Let $k$ be a number field and let $\G$ be a simple $k$-algebraic group. We call a subgroup $\Gamma \leq \G(k)$:

Algebraic
if there is a proper algebraic subgroup $\bbH \lneq \mathsf{Res}_{k/\Q}\, \G$ with $\Gamma \leq \bbH(\Q)$. These are the subgroups that are "cut out by equations": they fail to be Zariski dense after restriction of scalars.
Geometric
if there is a valuation $v$ on $k$ so that $\Gamma$ is discrete in $\G(k_v)$. These are the subgroups that act properly on some associated geometry — a symmetric space when $v$ is archimedean, a building when $v$ is non-archimedean.
Arithmetic
if there is a subring $A \leq k$ so that $\Gamma$ is commensurable with $\G(A)$. The prototype is $\SL_2(\Z) \leq \SL_2(\Q)$, and more generally the $S$-arithmetic groups like $\SL_2(\Z[1/p])$.

Many subgroup of $\G(k)$ may fall into more than one of these categories. For example, $\SL_2(\Z)$ is both geometric and arithmetic, while $\SL_2(\Z[1/p])$ is arithmetic but not geometric. But the conjecture is that no subgroup can avoid all three categories!

Conjecture

Every finitely generated subgroup of $\G(k)$ is either algebraic, geometric, or arithmetic.

In other words, a finitely generated subgroup of $\G(k)$ that is Zariski dense and not discrete in any completion should be forced to be a full group of $A$-points (well, up to commensurability at least).

Where did this conjecture come from?

Early in graduate school, I became rather obsessed with the following question: Can a surface group act properly on a locally compact product of trees? My advisor, Ian Agol, introduced me to the Long-Reid group in March 2018, which is a particular surface group contained in $\PSL_2(\Q)$. After struggling with this group for a while, I decided to try another approach to constructing such actions. It occurred to me that if one could find a surface group in $\SO_3(\Q)$, one would necessarily obtain the desired action on a product of trees. This lead me to consider the more general question of classifying subgroups of $\SO_3(\Q)$.
