## The figure-eight marble

This marble contains a finite view of a tiling of hyperbolic 3-space. Its central polyhedron is a Dirichlet domain for the figure-eight knot group. The surrounding colored cells are copies of that domain, moved by elements of the group. They fit together throughout hyperbolic space; the picture shows nearby copies inside the Poincaré ball model.

The figure-eight knot complement has a complete finite-volume hyperbolic structure. In one convention, its group is generated in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ by

$$A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\omega&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \omega=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}2.$$

A point and its images under the group determine the Dirichlet domain: keep the points at least as close to the chosen basepoint as to any other point of its orbit. Its faces pair under group elements. The visualization uses the domain computed by Poincaré, which reports that its face-pairing and edge-cycle checks pass; the group relations are checked exactly and the geometry numerically.

The sphere is the boundary at infinity of the ball model, rather than a physical glass surface. Hyperbolic space extends all the way to this boundary: the cells shrink in the picture as they approach it, even though their hyperbolic sizes are unchanged.

Drag to turn the marble; scroll or pinch to zoom. The default view rotates slowly. The rendered mesh is a finite set of nearby tiles, not the whole infinite tiling. Geometry and materials are captured from the Poincaré Tiling view (version 2026-11-05d) with the matrices above.
