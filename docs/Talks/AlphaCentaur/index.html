Part 2 · The question

Classification Conjecture

Let k be a number field and G an absolutely simple k-algebraic group. Call a subgroup Γ≤G(k)

  1. algebraic if Γ⊆H(Q) for a proper Q-subgroup H<Resk/Q​G;
  2. geometric if its image in G(kv​) is discrete for some nontrivial place v of k;
  3. arithmetic if Γ is commensurable with G(A) for some subring A⊆k.

Conjecture: Every subgroup is algebraic, geometric, or arithmetic!

The conjecture concerns every subgroup, not just finitely generated ones. The alternatives need not be exclusive: an arithmetic lattice can also be geometric. Here (Res_{k/Q} G)(Q) = G(k); H is a proper Q-algebraic subgroup of the restriction of scalars. G(A) is interpreted in a fixed faithful linear realization, with the usual commensurability convention for arithmetic subgroups. Distinguish this Q-Zariski closure from complex Zariski closure in PGL2 on the algebraic example slide.

Part 2 · For PGL₂

First observation

  • Finite generation gives Γ≤PGL2​(A) for a finitely generated ring A⊆Q​.
  • Choose A⊆Ok,S​, with k a number field and S a finite set of finite places.
  • Diagonally, Ok,S​ is a lattice in Rr×Cs×∏v∈S​kv​.

Conclusion

Γ↷(H2)r×(H3)s×v∈S∏​Tqv​+1​properly
Choose matrix representatives for the finite generating set and adjoin their entries and inverse determinants to Z. Enlarge this ring to O_{k,S}, with S finite and consisting of nonarchimedean places. The additive lattice assertion is about O_{k,S}, not an arbitrary subring A. The diagonal S-arithmetic group PGL2(O_{k,S}) is discrete in the product over all archimedean places and v in S. The action on the associated proper spaces has compact stabilizers, so its discrete subgroups act properly. Here r and s count real and complex places, and q_v is the size of the residue field at v; the Bruhat–Tits tree is (q_v+1)-regular.

Part 2 · Three kinds of subgroup

Algebraic subgroups

Proper complex Zariski closure in PGL2​:

finite or solvable.

The conjecture uses Q-closure: smaller fields and forms can also give proper subgroups.

For the ordinary complex Zariski closure inside PGL2(C), a proper algebraic subgroup is finite or solvable. The positive-dimensional cases lie in a Borel or the normalizer of a torus (including their subgroups). This is a narrower assertion than the conjecture, whose ambient group is Res_{k/Q} G: proper Q-closures can also arise by descent to a smaller field or a form. For example, PGL2(Q) in PGL2(k), with k properly containing Q, has proper Q-closure after restriction of scalars but is neither solvable nor compact. The next slide illustrates the Borel case.

Part 2 · Step 1, not dense

Not dense: an explicit structure

1 · Borel closure

Γ fixes a point of P1, so it acts by z↦λz+b.

2 · A semidirect product

Γ≅Z[61​]⋊3/2​Z: translations, with t acting by multiplication by 3/2.

3 · Finitely presented?

No (Bieri–Strebel). The relation tu2t−1=u3 defines BS(2,3), which surjects onto Γ.

Live: Z[61​]⋊Z

Take u(z)=z+1 and t(z)=(3/2)z. Their translation subgroup is Z[3/2,2/3]=Z[1/6], giving Gamma = Z[1/6] semidirect Z. This is an exact structural description, not an ordinary presentation with fractional powers. The ordinary one-relator group <u,t | t u^2 t^{-1}=u^3> is BS(2,3). Its matrix image is the metabelian quotient Gamma; the kernel is nontrivial (for example the image satisfies [u,tut^{-1}]=1). Checking proposed relators on matrices establishes only a homomorphism, not injectivity or completeness of the relator list. Bieri–Strebel implies Gamma is not finitely presented. Reference: de Cornulier and de la Harpe, Amenable groups without finitely presented amenable covers, https://doi.org/10.1007/s13373-013-0031-5, discussion of Met(2,3); reversing the cyclic generator exchanges 2/3 and 3/2.

Throughout this slide Gamma is finitely generated and acts properly. On a locally finite tree it is virtually free; in H2 it has a torsion-free subgroup of finite index that is a surface group (including free groups). In H3, Selberg’s lemma gives a torsion-free subgroup of finite index, and Scott’s compact core theorem gives finite presentability. Bass–Serre theory and the surface classification handle the first two rows. The algorithmic question for H3 here concerns finitely many matrices with algebraic entries; no claim is made about an unspecified computational model for arbitrary complex inputs.

Part 2 · Improving the calculator

Algorithm Self-Improvement Algorithm

Sample

Run at a fixed budget; check certificates.

Study

Compare hard cases with longer runs, and find better tuning constants

Improve

Develop a general method for the tricky ones

Test

Compare on fresh samples; keep sound gains.

Goal: understand the remaining cases well enough to prove a theorem!

ASIA: Algorithm Self-Improvement Algorithm. Sample a distribution of groups and run the current algorithm at a fixed working budget. Among groups it leaves undecided, separate reachable examples that a larger budget resolves from stubborn examples that remain undecided. Study the largest reachable group, compare the two runs, diagnose the common bottleneck, and develop a general improvement. Compare old and new algorithms on the same fresh sample at the same budget; independently check certificates, reject disagreements, and accept only an evidence-backed gain in coverage or speed. Record both successful and failed attempts. The mathematical aim is to characterize the remaining undecided cases and ultimately prove a termination theorem or identify a failure family. This is the method; the preceding index-2304 example is not claimed as a particular ASIA iteration.

Congruence closure

1 · Prime by prime

For Λ=PGL2​(Z[31​]), the exceptional levels here are 5,7,13.

2 · One finite quotient

The congruence closure Δ of Γ in Λ has [Λ:Δ]=611,520.

3 · Congruence subgroups

Here S={3}=∅. CSP gives [Λ:Γ]<∞ exactly when Γ=Δ.

Live: the Long–Reid group at t=9

Here Lambda=PGL2(Z[1/3]), Gamma is the displayed Long–Reid subgroup, and Delta is its full congruence closure in Lambda, including the determinant square classes. Delta has finite index in this Zariski-dense example. The congruence subgroup property for this rational S-integer ambient group, with S={3} nonempty, says every finite-index subgroup contains a principal congruence subgroup (equivalently, is congruence closed). Thus [Lambda:Gamma] is finite iff Gamma=Delta. Always [Lambda:Gamma] >= [Lambda:Delta]. This equivalence uses the true closure: a computation at only some primes or levels supplies an upper bound on Delta and a lower bound on index unless completeness is certified. The Long–Reid group is discrete at the real place, whereas no finite-index subgroup of Lambda is, so it has infinite index and is thin. Reference: Serre, Le problème des groupes de congruence pour SL2, Annals of Mathematics 92 (1970), 489–527; account at https://www.mathi.uni-heidelberg.de/~wienhard/retreat10/references/raghunathan_csp.pdf. The implementation accounts for PGL2 determinant classes in PresentationCalc/js/congruence.js.
