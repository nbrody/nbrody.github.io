How to keep the colony alive

P

Callows & nymphs (the pale, cyan ones) need proctodeal fluid: hindgut liquid straight from your rear end. Turn around and back into them — hold Shift to reverse without turning.

S

Soldiers, larvae and the queen can't feed themselves at all. They get stomodeal food from your crop, mouth to mouth. Face them head-on.

▮

Gnaw wood to load your crop (amber). Each amber dose ferments into green hindgut fluid after about five seconds — so keep both on hand, because green won't feed a soldier and amber won't re-seed a callow.

✕

Ant raiders break in from the tunnel mouths. You can't fight them — but a well-fed soldier will. Keep their bars up and they'll do the killing.

This is a real thing, unfortunately

Lower termites can't digest wood on their own. The work is done by a zoo of flagellate protozoa living in the hindgut — and at every moult a termite sheds its gut lining and loses the entire culture. A freshly moulted callow is a termite with a stomach full of sawdust and nothing to ferment it, and it will starve surrounded by food unless a nestmate re-inoculates it.

The re-inoculation is proctodeal trophallaxis: the donor everts its hindgut and the recipient drinks a droplet directly from its anus. It is a genuine, load-bearing piece of colony infrastructure. The same colonies also run stomodeal trophallaxis — regurgitated crop food passed mouth to mouth — which is how soldiers, whose mandibles are too specialised to eat with, and the queen, and the youngest larvae are fed. Two pipelines, two ends, and a worker caste spending its life shuttling between them.

The scoring, the ant raids and the moult timing are invented. The two feeding routes, the caste that receives each one, and the reason callows need the rear one are not.
