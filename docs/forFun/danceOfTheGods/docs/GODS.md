# DANCE OF THE GODS — The Twelve

Character sheets for the twelve Olympians of the game — one per domain of the
type chart. This file describes **who they are**: temperament, bearing, what
pleases and offends them, and how they stand to one another. It tells **no
stories**; every tale belongs to `myths.md`. When a god speaks in dialogue,
this is the document that says how.

**A note on the roster.** The canonical lists of "the Twelve" vary; ours is
the twelve *domains*. We seat Hades (the underworld is a third of the world)
and Demeter and Dionysus, and we do not seat Hera or Hestia — Hestia because
she would never leave the hearth to appear in a road game, and Hera because
she is more interesting as an off-screen gravity that several of the others
orbit. Both may yet appear; neither has a beast line.

## The family, briefly

- **The elder generation** — children of Cronus and Rhea, who divided the
  world by lot: **Zeus** (sky), **Poseidon** (sea), **Hades** (below), and
  their sisters **Demeter** and Hera and Hestia. All six are siblings; every
  other Olympian is their child, and almost all of those are Zeus's.
- **Zeus's children**: **Athena** (born of Zeus alone, from the head — her
  mother Metis was wisdom itself, swallowed); the twins **Apollo** and
  **Artemis** (by Leto); **Ares** and **Hephaestus** (by Hera — though some
  say Hera made Hephaestus alone, out of spite, which both of them believe on
  alternating days); **Hermes** (by the nymph Maia); **Dionysus** (by mortal
  Semele — the only Olympian with a mortal mother, and the last seated).
- **Aphrodite** stands outside the tree: older than it, foam-born of the sky's
  first wound — though Homer politely calls her a daughter of Zeus. She is
  married to **Hephaestus** and drawn to **Ares**, an arrangement everyone on
  the mountain knows about and no one mentions at dinner.
- **Persephone**, Demeter's daughter by Zeus, is queen below beside **Hades**
  half the year — which makes Hades both Demeter's brother and her son-in-law,
  a fact Demeter has never once said out loud.

## Zeus — Sky

**Bearing.** The room rearranges itself when he enters; he assumes it will and
is right. Enormous, genial, terrifying — the geniality and the terror are not
different moods but the same weather at different distances.

**Temper.** Quick to wrath, quicker to amnesty; his grudges are rare but
geological. He is the only Olympian who thinks about the *whole* — order,
oaths, guests, the line between gods and Titans — and the loneliness of that
office makes him by turns magnanimous and self-indulgent. He keeps the law and
exempts himself from it, and knows he does, and dares anyone to raise it.

**Pleased by** audacity that respects him; oaths kept; a well-run city; being
asked for judgment. **Offended by** hubris (the specific crime of forgetting
the distance between mortal and god), broken guest-right, and being managed.

**Relations.** Brother to Poseidon and Hades, and their arbiter, which both
resent in different keys. Father of half the pantheon. He trusts Athena's
counsel above anyone's, indulges Hermes like a favorite pocketknife, finds
Ares embarrassing, Apollo a little too shiny, and Dionysus inexplicable but
good at parties.

## Poseidon — Sea

**Bearing.** Second lot, and never once forgot it. Broad, bearded, barnacled
in old dignities; moves like weather coming in. He does not enter rooms — he
arrives on coasts.

**Temper.** The longest grudge-keeper on the mountain, and the most honestly
affectionate when pleased — sailors know both faces in one afternoon. Prickly
about honor, generous with his own people, incapable of pretending a feeling
he does not have. His angers are not tantrums; they are tides, scheduled and
total.

**Pleased by** horses, harbors, libations poured neat into the water, and
being *asked first*. **Offended by** walls built without his blessing, rivals
honored on his coastlines, and any implication that the sea is a road rather
than a kingdom.

**Relations.** Zeus's elder brother, ranked beneath him, and salted by it.
Rival of Athena in a standing, almost professional way — the two of them
contest cities and respect each other more than either admits. Fond uncle to
none in particular; father of storms, horses, and far too many giants.

## Hades — Underworld

**Bearing.** The quietest of the three brothers and the only one whose realm
runs on time. Pale, still, courteous with the courtesy of a man who never
needs to raise his voice because everyone arrives eventually.

**Temper.** Not evil, not cruel, not even grim — *exact*. He is the keeper of
the one law with no exceptions, and it has made him honest past the point of
comfort. He does not lie, does not haggle twice, and does not forgive escapes.
Below the exactness: a deep, embarrassed capacity for tenderness that he
administrates like everything else, carefully.

**Pleased by** kept bargains, respect paid to the dead, pomegranates,
silence that isn't fear. **Offended by** necromancy, heroes treating his
kingdom as a errand-stop, and pity.

**Relations.** Eldest brother of Zeus and Poseidon, absent from Olympus by
preference — he considers the mountain loud. Husband of Persephone, whose
half-year absence he keeps like an altar. Demeter does not speak to him;
he sends the first fruits up every spring anyway. Hermes is the only Olympian
with standing permission to enter, and the only one Hades would call, with
private precision, a friend.

## Athena — Wisdom

**Bearing.** Grey-eyed, unhurried, always slightly better prepared than the
situation required. Armor worn like formal dress; a spear carried like a
signature.

**Temper.** The strategist, not the scholar — her wisdom is *applied*: cities,
ships, looms, campaigns. Calm as policy, but her pride is the true steel; she
forgives errors of ability instantly and errors of presumption never. She
plays favorites among mortals and calls it curriculum.

**Pleased by** craft mastered, plans that survive contact, questions asked
before rather than after. **Offended by** boasts against the gods, waste of
talent, and being mistaken for a war god — Ares is *war*; she is *winning*.

**Relations.** Zeus's first and favorite, born of his own head, and the only
one who can tell him no at full volume. Standing rival of Poseidon; standing
contempt for Ares (it is mutual and keeps them both sharp). She finds
Hephaestus restful — the two makers understand each other — and Aphrodite
professionally baffling.

## Ares — War

**Bearing.** Magnificent and slightly too loud, like a bronze bell struck in
a small room. Beautiful the way fire is; welcome the way fire is.

**Temper.** Not strategy — *charge*. Ares is the joy of battle and its horror
carried in the same body, honest as a wound. He is the least loved Olympian
and knows it, which he armors in swagger; underneath is something startling: a
capacity for absolute, undivided loyalty to whoever stands with him. He never
schemes. He is the only Olympian who never schemes.

**Pleased by** courage anywhere (he honors brave enemies), loyalty, dogs,
being chosen first for once. **Offended by** cowardice, poisoners, war made
with ledgers, jokes about the net.

**Relations.** Son of Zeus and Hera and somehow the disappointment of both.
Despises Athena's cold victories; she returns fire with interest. Bound to
Aphrodite by the oldest attraction on the mountain — war and love, each sure
they've conquered the other. Genuinely kind, when unobserved, to Hephaestus's
limp and Hermes's chatter.

## Aphrodite — Love

**Bearing.** The eye goes to her; that is not vanity but physics. Foam-pale,
sea-eyed, dressed in whatever the beholder was already dreaming of. She
laughs easily and means it.

**Temper.** Mistaken for soft by everyone exactly once. Aphrodite is an
elemental power wearing charm — older than Olympus, and beneath the warmth
runs the sea she came from: unaccountable, tidal, occasionally ruthless. She
is generous to lovers, merciless to those who mock love, and entirely
uninterested in being reasonable about either.

**Pleased by** devotion, doves, honest desire, good stories about bad
decisions. **Offended by** vows of celibacy announced in her hearing, love
used as a weapon by anyone but her, and being told to calm down.

**Relations.** Outside the family tree and senior to it; married by
arrangement to Hephaestus, magnetized to Ares, and unrepentant about the
geometry. Fond of Hermes (they trade gossip like currency), patient with
Dionysus, wary of Artemis, and locked in eternal polite non-agreement with
Athena about what mortals are for.

## Apollo — Sun

**Bearing.** Golden, unbearably so; the best archer, the best musician, the
best-looking, and aware of each ranking. Radiance as a manner: he doesn't
enter conversations, he dawns on them.

**Temper.** The most gifted and the most brittle of the twelve. Apollo is
order, clarity, prophecy, healing, music — everything luminous — and his
shadow is exactly that light turned possessive: he cannot lose gracefully,
cannot be refused safely, and cannot understand why not. His griefs are
operatic and sincere. He is very young, forever, in the way only the sun can
be: every dawn convinced this time will be different.

**Pleased by** music (he will stop mid-sentence for it), truth told
beautifully, laurel, being *seen*. **Offended by** false prophets,
challenges to his artistry, and — most dangerously — laughter at his
expense.

**Relations.** Twin of Artemis, younger by minutes and reminded of it for
millennia; she is the one being he defers to. Son of Zeus and Leto. He and
Hermes maintain a friendship founded on a trade neither regrets. Toward
Orpheus specifically: complicated — the god of music regarding the mortal
who might be better at the only thing that matters.

## Artemis — Hunt

**Bearing.** Moon-silver and utterly self-contained; the stillness of a drawn
bow. She is most herself at tree-line, at dusk, at distance.

**Temper.** Freedom made a person. Artemis asks nothing from anyone and
extends the same courtesy as a kind of fierce respect: she does not rescue,
she does not meddle, she watches to see what you do. Her law is simple —
wild things are not property — and her enforcement is immediate and without
appeal. With her chosen companions she is warm as a campfire; the warmth is
never transferable.

**Pleased by** clean kills, released catches, oaths kept to the letter,
silence in the woods. **Offended by** trespass on her privacy, cruelty to
animals, hunters who take more than they carry, and anyone who touches her
companions.

**Relations.** Twin and elder of Apollo — the only person allowed to tease
him. Daughter of Zeus and Leto. She respects Athena, tolerates Hermes,
avoids Aphrodite as one avoids weather, and regards Ares's hunting the way a
surgeon regards an axe. Demeter is the aunt she actually visits.

## Hephaestus — Forge

**Bearing.** Soot-blackened, burn-scarred, lame in both legs and utterly
unbothered on his own ground; in the forge his ugliness resolves into a kind
of gravity. Rolled shoulders, clever hands, a voice pitched under the hammer's
ring.

**Temper.** The only Olympian who *works*. Patient in the way of processes
that cannot be rushed, kind in the way of people who have been hurt and
decided against passing it on — and possessed, deep down, of the mountain's
slowest and most inventive anger: he forgives injuries to himself eventually,
and humiliations never quickly. His revenges are devices. They have release
mechanisms.

**Pleased by** apprentices, problems ("bring me a *problem*"), automata,
being invited rather than summoned. **Offended by** shoddy work praised,
his gifts melted down, mockery of his gait, and pity — the sole insult he
shares with Hades.

**Relations.** Son of Hera (and of Zeus, on the days they claim each other);
thrown from the mountain once and walked back up, which is his entire
biography in one clause. Married to Aphrodite — a wound he has machined into
something like wry peace. Ares is his opposite and occasional victim.
Athena is his colleague; Dionysus, unexpectedly, his best friend — the god
of wine being the only one who ever came to *get* him.

## Hermes — Herald

**Bearing.** Already here. You didn't see him arrive and won't see him leave;
winged sandals, a traveler's hat, and the grin of someone holding a joke
you'll get later. He looks lightweight. So does a key.

**Temper.** Motion, exchange, threshold — the god of everything that crosses:
borders, markets, messages, souls. He lies fluently and keeps faith
scrupulously, and sees no contradiction, because the lies are play and the
trust is business. The kindest Olympian to mortals in practice, precisely
because he expects nothing of them but company on the road. His neutrality is
real (the chart makes it mechanical: never super-effective, never resisted) —
and it is a *choice*, renewed daily, which is the most Hermetic thing about
him.

**Pleased by** wit, fair trades that were also somehow unfair, first
inventions, travelers who share their food, tortoises. **Offended by**
almost nothing — which the observant find more unnerving than wrath. Do not
cheat a guest under his eye.

**Relations.** Youngest son of Zeus by Maia; his father's messenger,
fixer, and confessor. Friend to Apollo by treaty, to Hades by standing
appointment (the only god welcome below), to everyone else by default. He is
the pantheon's connective tissue — sibling-adjacent to all, sworn to none.
He gave Orpheus his first companion, and gods do not do things once.

## Demeter — Harvest

**Bearing.** Broad-handed, wheat-crowned, unhurried; the presence of a
harvest-season noon. She is the Olympian most often found *standing in a
field*, and the one mortals mistake for mortal most often — she takes it as
a compliment.

**Temper.** The deepest love on the mountain and the proof of its cost. Demeter
is sustenance itself: patient as seasons, generous as soil — and her grief,
when struck, does not rage; it *withholds*, and the whole world starves with
her. She keeps the oldest law of give-and-return, and she is done, forever,
with being told to be reasonable about her daughter.

**Pleased by** bread shared, first fruits offered, fields left fallow in
their year, mothers. **Offended by** hoarding in famine, salted earth,
and the mention of certain arrangements as though they were settled.

**Relations.** Sister of Zeus, Poseidon, Hades — and mother of Persephone by
the first, which the third made into the family's permanent winter. She does
not attend councils in the cold months. Artemis and Hephaestus she treats as
favorite nephews-in-spirit; Hermes she tolerates because he carries her
messages *down*. Dionysus is her strange younger echo — grain and vine, the
two gods mortals actually need.

## Dionysus — Wine

**Bearing.** Ivy-crowned, wine-dark-eyed, barefoot at every altitude; the
youngest Olympian and the only one who remembers being mortal-born. Around him
edges soften — of cups, of certainties, of selves. His panthers purr like
distant thunder.

**Temper.** The god of *loosening*: wine, theater, ecstasy, the mask that
tells the truth. Gentle past all expectation with the broken and the
grieving — and the most genuinely dangerous Olympian when refused, because
what he loosens, refused, is loosened anyway, badly. He is disorder as mercy,
and his standing question to every tight-wound soul is the same: *what are
you holding, and what is it costing you to hold it?*

**Pleased by** hospitality, festivals, honest tears, masks worn knowingly,
his mother's name spoken kindly. **Offended by** kings who ban the vine,
piety worn as armor, and anyone who calls the theater a lie.

**Relations.** Son of Zeus and mortal Semele; twice-born, last-seated, and
serenely unbothered by both. He fetched Hephaestus home when no god would,
which made the two of them permanent. Demeter is his elder counterpart in the
economy of what mortals live on. Apollo is his opposite number — form and
flood, lyre and drum — and their rivalry is secretly a duet. To Orpheus he
stands nearest of all the twelve, patron of the singer's art and its abyss,
and the game should let that nearness feel like both a blessing and a
warning.
