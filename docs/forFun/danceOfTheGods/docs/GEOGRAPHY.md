# DANCE OF THE GODS — Geography

Every place Orpheus walks, sails, or descends through: the seven maps already
built, the regions the myths demand, and three new lands folded into the plan
by request — **the siren waters**, **the deep forest of Artemis**, and
**Circe's island, Aeaea**. Each entry gives the real-world/canon anchor, the
look (tiles & palette in the black-figure idiom), the inhabitants, what is
staged there, and its status.

Format of the world: a **land road** running Pimpleia → Delphi → Athens →
Corinth (built), continuing Corinth → Sparta → Eleusis (planned); a **sea
ring** opening out of Corinth's harbor (new); and the two **vertical ends** —
the Underworld below Eleusis, Olympus above it all.

```
                       MT. OLYMPUS  (XII)
                            ▲
   Pimpleia ─ Grove Road ─ Delphi ═ Sacred Way ═ Athens ─ Coast Road ─ Corinth
   (start)      (Kass)     (I)      (IX told)    (II)                  (III·XI)
                                       │                                  │
                                    Eleusis (VII) ── Underworld (VIII)    │ washed-out road (X gate)
                                       ▲                                  ▼
                                  The Deep Green ── Taygetos ─ Sparta (V) ─ Forge Cave (X)
                                  (VI · Artemis)
                                                                          ═ sea ═
                             Corinth harbor ─ Siren Straits ─ Aeaea ─ legendary isles
                                                (Argo song)   (Circe)   (BEASTS.md)
```
`─` built or directly planned · `═` gated by a myth · Roman numerals = myths.md

## PART I — The Road So Far (implemented)

### Pimpleia — the Village Beneath Olympus
**Anchor.** Pieria, at the mountain's northern foot; Orpheus's canonical
birthplace, home of the Muses' springs.
**Look.** Terracotta ground, two houses, the well, flower clusters; the
quietest palette in the game — this is what "safe" looks like, so everywhere
else can look like not-this.
**Who.** Elder Myrrha (healer, keeper of the Calliope references), the
village child, and — until the gift is given — Hermes at the north gate.
**What happens.** The Gift of Hermes (Myth IX's opening cameo). Later, per
myths.md VIII: the letters, the flowers by the well, the elder at the gate —
Pimpleia is where the Eurydice thread is seeded and where it breaks.
**Status.** Built.

### Grove Road
**Anchor.** The olive-and-scrub country of Thessaly's edges.
**Look.** Tall-grass fields, a pond, one rock chokepoint.
**Who.** Kass of the Cult of Typhon (first rival fight); wild beasts Lv 4–7.
**What happens.** First catches, first grind, the sealed Delphi gate.
**Status.** Built.

### Delphi — Navel of the World
**Anchor.** The sanctuary on Parnassus; omphalos, temple terrace, the spring.
**Look.** Marble plaza and colonnade against terracotta; the slain Python on
the stones; the river Peneus (poetic license — moved from Thessaly to carry
the Daphne scene); the young laurel after Myth I.
**Who.** Apollo, Eros, Daphne, Pythia (healer), a Temple Acolyte (trainer).
**What happens.** **Myth I.** Completion parts the Sacred Way behind the
temple.
**Status.** Built.

### The Sacred Way
**Anchor.** The pilgrim road through Boeotia, walked east-to-west in history;
ours runs the other way.
**Look.** A ridge road: grass shelves, a mountain pond, long sightlines.
**Who.** Rhapsode Phemios (tells Myth IX), Pilgrim Iona (trainer); wild Lv 5–9
skewing Sky/Wisdom.
**Status.** Built.

### Athens — City of the Olive (née Cecropia)
**Anchor.** The acropolis rock and the asty below it.
**Look.** The game's grandest marble: temple, four-column front, agora
houses; the salt spring and the first olive tree remain on the rock forever
after Myth II.
**Who.** King Cecrops, Athena, Poseidon, Priestess Aglauros (healer),
Philosopher Timon (trainer), citizens.
**What happens.** **Myth II**; the city is named on screen. Future per plan:
the Parthenon riddle-gym. Athens is also the natural stage door to the Deep
Green (Narcissus is Boeotian; the forest hangs off this stretch of the map).
**Status.** Built (gym planned).

### The Coast Road
**Anchor.** The Saronic shore road, Megara-way.
**Look.** Grass shelves over a glittering edge of sea; first taste of salt
palette.
**Who.** Hoplite Drakon, Fisher Halios (trainers); wild Lv 8–11 skewing
Sea/Forge/War.
**Status.** Built.

### Corinth — Mistress of Two Seas
**Anchor.** The isthmus city: two harbors, the Pirene fountain, Acrocorinth
with Aphrodite's temple brooding above.
**Look.** Agora and fountain; the Lechaion harbor band with its dock — the
game's first standing water you can walk beside.
**Who.** Priestess of Aphrodite (healer; teases Myth XI), Bellerophon
(dreaming at Pirene; Myth III), Bosun Okypete (trainer), merchant.
**What happens.** Corinth is the hinge of the whole map: **Myth III**
(Pegasus) begins here, **Myth XI** (the Golden Net) plays in the temple above,
the washed-out west road is the **Myth X** gate to Sparta, and the harbor
opens the **sea ring** below.
**Status.** Built (myths staged: teasers only).

## PART II — The Land Ahead (planned)

### The Forge Cave — under the Sparta road
**Anchor.** No fixed canon site — a smith's cave in the Corinthian hills;
Lemnos-by-proxy.
**Look.** First interior map: dark basalt tiles, ember-vent glow, the trapped
golden throne glinting. Bronze accent everywhere (#e0914d earning its keep).
**Who.** Hephaestus (sulking), Hera's priestess, a singed hoplite; Forge-line
wilds (Cindercrab nests).
**What happens.** **Myth X** — the Return of Hephaestus. Completion unblocks
the road to Sparta: the geography is literally jammed by the family quarrel.
**Status.** Planned.

### Sparta — the Agoge
**Anchor.** The Eurotas valley under Taygetos.
**Look.** Austere: no marble luxury, packed-earth training yards, spear racks,
the great bronze jar in the courtyard. Palette drops to iron and blood-red.
**Who.** Polemarch Leon (gauntlet gym per DESIGN.md), the priest of Ares, the
boasting Aloadae twins (giant NPCs), Kass (rival fight #3).
**What happens.** **Myth V** — Ares in the Bronze Jar; then the five-fight
gauntlet with no healing.
**Status.** Planned.

### Taygetos & the Deep Green — the forest of Artemis  *(expanded by request)*
**Anchor.** A consolidation, and the game's one great forest region: the
Ceryneian hill country (Myth VI) grown into a full wood that also borrows
Pelion's centaurs and Boeotia's Narcissus pool. One forest to hold everything
the myths keep in trees.
**Look.** The darkest greens in the game — canopy tiles that shade the path,
god-rays through gaps, tall grass replaced by *fern* tiles with their own
encounter table. Deeper in, the light stops being reliable: the Deep Green
runs at permanent dusk, fireflies as the only accent. No roads — deer-trails
that fork and re-fork (soft maze; the map itself doesn't want to be mapped).
**Who.**
- **Artemis**, glimpsed long before met: a silver figure at tree-line who is
  never there when you arrive. She speaks only at the climax of Myth VI.
- **The golden hind**, one map ahead of you, always (the chase mechanic).
- **Centaurs** at a clearing camp — rowdy, wine-starved, spoiling for
  trainer fights (War/Wild teams) — and apart from them **Chiron** in a cave
  mouth: healer, lore-keeper, the one NPC who can name every beast in your
  amphorae and say something true about each (a living BEASTS.md).
- **Narcissus** at a still black pool: a beautiful NPC who cannot be spoken
  to — every dialogue attempt returns his own line back to the player,
  slightly wrong. The pool is a mirror set-piece: stand at its edge and the
  game renders *your* sprite reflected where a beast should be. (Echo is
  here too: a sign that repeats the last thing any NPC said to you.)
**What happens.** **Myth VI** (the Hind and the release test) is the spine.
The centaur camp is the optional trainer-gauntlet; Chiron is the forest's
sanctuary. Narcissus is a side-tale, not a numbered myth — a quiet warning
about looking at yourself when you should be looking at the world, placed
deliberately on the road to Eleusis and the mirror-choice of Myth VIII.
**Encounters.** Hunt/Harvest/Wine wilds Lv 14–18 (Fawnling line's home);
ferns only.
**Status.** New — adopted into the plan (absorbs the old "Taygetos road").

### Eleusis — the Mysteries
**Anchor.** The Telesterion and the Rarian plain, west of Athens.
**Look.** Torchlit, half-underground; the fields around it *change with the
myth* — green tiles wither to bare earth during Demeter's grief and regreen
at the reunion. The one map whose palette is a plot.
**Who.** Hierophant Nyx (dual-domain gym per DESIGN.md), Demeter, the torch
procession.
**What happens.** **Myth VII**; completion opens the cave mouth below.
**Status.** Planned.

### The Underworld
**Anchor.** Below; entered from Eleusis (with a nod to Taenarum, the
traditional singer's door).
**Look.** The palette inverts — cream ground, ink sky; the one map drawn as
the *back* of a vase. The Styx as a moving band; asphodel as grey flower
tiles; the three great shades as set-piece rooms.
**Who.** Charon, Cerberos (soothed, not fought), Hades and Persephone
enthroned, the listening dead.
**What happens.** **Myth VIII.** The Look Back input. This is the bottom of
the game in every sense.
**Status.** Planned.

### Mount Olympus
**Anchor.** The summit above Pimpleia — the game ends a day's walk from where
it began, straight up.
**Look.** Weather-zone bands climbed in sequence (storm, snowfield, cloud-sea,
gold summit terrace); the Olympian Gate; the Divine Council's hall.
**Who.** The Divine Council (elite four per DESIGN.md), Kass (final rival
resolution), the Cult of Typhon at the un-pinning.
**What happens.** **Myth XII**, sung rather than won.
**Status.** Planned.

## PART III — The Sea Ring (new, by request)

Opened from Corinth's Lechaion dock after Myth IV (Dionysus & the Pirates)
supplies the ferryman: the spared helmsman runs the routes. Sea travel is a
boarded-ship overworld variant: the ship is the walkable map; ports and
set-pieces come to *it*.

### The Siren Straits — the waterworld
**Anchor.** The song-haunted narrows of Argonautica IV and Odyssey XII,
placed between the gulf and the open Aegean.
**Look.** The game's first all-water region: wave tiles in three blues, reef
rocks, wreck timbers, gull-wheeled skerries. The ship-map threads a channel;
the **siren rocks** rise on either side, white with old bones and unbothered
gulls.
**Who.**
- **Nereids** — the "mermaids" of the Greek sea (Nereus's daughters), rising
  at the rail in the calm stretches: friendly, curious, gift-giving; a
  Nereid grotto serves as the region's healer and its gossip-well (they know
  every myth's status and comment accordingly).
- **Tritons** — conch-blowing wave-herders; the straits' trainers, fought
  rail-to-rail (Sea/Herald teams).
- **The Sirens** — three, named as in the old lists (Parthenope, Leucosia,
  Ligeia): not a battle but a **song-duel set-piece**. In canon it was
  Orpheus, aboard the Argo, who out-sang them — the game leans on that
  directly: a rhythm/dialogue beat where the player's lyre must hold the
  crew's attention against the sirens' pull. Win, and one siren, the
  youngest, asks — quietly, off-myth — what the mainland is like now.
**What happens.** The Argo song-duel (a side-tale, not a numbered myth — it
is Orpheus's own résumé); passage control for the whole sea ring; Sea-domain
wilds Lv 16–20 breaching alongside (Calfin line's deep-water cousins).
**Status.** New — adopted into the plan.

### Aeaea — Circe's Island
**Anchor.** Odyssey X: the low wooded isle, the house of polished stone in a
clearing, smoke above the trees.
**Look.** Deceptively gentle: parkland tiles, tame flowers, a column of hearth
smoke always on the horizon — and every animal drawn *slightly wrong for its
species*: pigs with too-knowing eyes, lions that beg like dogs, wolves that
weep. The wrongness is the region's tell, and it is done entirely with the
eye-rendering (spEye pupils tracking the player).
**Who.** **Circe** — pharmakis, weaver, aunt of the Minotaur's mother,
perfectly hospitable and perfectly dangerous; her transformed court; one
un-transformed sailor hiding on the beach who begs you not to drink anything.
**What happens.** The island is the game's darkest riff on its own core
mechanic: Circe also collects beasts — hers used to be people. A capture-ethics
set-piece: her "wilds" can be caught like any beast, but Chiron, the Nereids,
and your own Dovie all react to a transformed man in your amphorae, and the
endgame offers their release. Circe herself is an optional grand trainer
(Wine/Wisdom/Sea team, the sea ring's hardest fight) and, defeated or
befriended, becomes a healer who brews the game's status-cure items. A
side-tale, not a numbered myth — but it rhymes with VI (what may be bound?)
and foreshadows VIII (what may be brought back?).
**Status.** New — adopted into the plan.

### The Legendary Isles
**Anchor & function.** The far ring: small single-purpose islets hiding the
legendaries per BEASTS.md — Lerna's marsh (Hydra), the sky above the straits
(Pegasus, never landed), Etna's far glow (Phoenix, endgame). Each is one
screen, one encounter, one held breath.
**Status.** Planned.

## Design notes

- **Regions are palettes.** Every region owns one palette shift (marble,
  iron-red, deep green, inverted underworld, three blues) so a screenshot is
  identifiable at a glance. The vase-band frame never changes; only the world
  inside it.
- **New tiles implied**: canopy/fern (Deep Green), basalt/ember (Forge Cave),
  withering fields (Eleusis), wave/reef/ship (sea ring), asphodel/inverted
  ground (Underworld). Each region should add at most a handful — the tile
  legend in `../js/overworld.js` stays readable.
- **Myth gates make the map.** Every `═` edge in the graph is opened by a
  story beat, never by a key item: X opens Sparta, IV opens the sea, VII opens
  the deep door. The world unlocks by being witnessed.
- **The three requested additions** (Siren Straits, the Deep Green, Aeaea)
  slot in without new myth numbers: the sirens and Circe are *Orpheus-facing
  side-tales* (his own legend, not the gods'), and the forest absorbs Myth VI
  whole. Athens and Olympus were already seated (Myths II and XII).
