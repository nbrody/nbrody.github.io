Guide

A web is an oriented planar trivalent graph in a disk whose edges carry labels, with flow conserved at every vertex: exactly for gl(N) (MOY) webs, modulo N for sl(N), where a k-edge is the same as a reversed (N−k)-edge, so sl₃ webs are Kuperberg's bipartite webs. A foam between webs W₀ and W₁ is a 2-dimensional complex in D² × [0, 1] whose horizontal slices are webs: labelled surfaces (facets) meeting three at a time along bindings, which meet four at a time at singular vertices.

Editing the webs

  • Use the W₀ and W₁ tabs of the web card. Draw by dragging: start or end on a boundary point, a vertex, an edge (it gets split) or empty space; drag across the circle to make boundary points. Edges point the way you drag; a closed loop becomes a circle.
  • Boundary points are shared by both webs: they are the vertical boundary of the foam. Number keys set the label (hover an edge to relabel it), r reverses the hovered edge, Delete removes what is under the cursor.
  • Problems (dangling edges, flow violations, crossings) are ringed in red and listed in the status banner.

Knotted webs

Webs need not be planar: draw an edge across another and it makes a crossing (the new strand over, or under — see the option under the tools); the Crossing tool switches it. Such a web lives in D² × [−1, 1], and a foam between two of them lives in D² × [−1, 1] × [0, 1]. The 3D view then shows its projection to D² × [0, 1], with dashed double lines where sheets pass through each other, and Web in space (on the bottom bar) plays the movie: the web moving through space from W₀ to W₁, depth exaggerated.

Building the foam

The foam is a movie: W₀ = F₀ followed by elementary foams, each a generic critical event of the height function. Pick a frame in the Movie list (or with ↑ ↓), choose a move in the web card and click in the frame; hovering previews the result. New moves go directly above the selected frame, so you can change the foam anywhere along its height.

  • Cup / Cap: a minimum / maximum of a facet — a circle is born / dies.
  • Saddle: a saddle point of a facet, between two oppositely oriented strands across a region.
  • Zip / Unzip: a minimum / maximum of a binding. Zip merges two neighbouring strands into an H; unzip opens an H whose legs pair up.
  • Digon ±, Theta ±: the other binding extrema — an edge grows or loses a digon, a theta web is born or dies.
  • I ↔ H: a singular vertex (associativity of trivalent vertices).
  • Isotopy: drag things in a frame; this inserts an isotopy step and reshapes everything above it.
  • Slide: drag a strand over everything else (shift: under). This is an isotopy in space and covers Reidemeister II, III and IV; crossings appear and disappear as the strand passes. Kink ± are Reidemeister I; Twist ± is Reidemeister V (two legs at a vertex trade places, crossing once).
  • Dot: decorate a facet (click an edge in a frame, or a facet in the scene; shift-click removes).

When the last frame is isotopic to W₁ (rel boundary) a final isotopy is added automatically and the foam is complete; otherwise W₁ floats dashed above the foam and over the last frame. Steps can be deleted (✕) or swapped with the one below (⇅); steps that stop making sense after an edit are struck through until you fix or delete them.

Reading the foam

Drag the height slider at the bottom (or press ▶) to sweep a plane through the foam; the web card then shows the true level set. The Facets tab lists every facet with its label, Euler characteristic χ, genus g, boundary circles ∂ and dots, computed from the triangulated surfaces themselves.
