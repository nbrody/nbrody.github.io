Theta-Graph Product Without the Diagonal

The construction

Start with two theta graphs, each carrying one red, one green, and one blue edge. Their product has nine square cells ec × ed. Delete the three diagonal squares, and the remaining six cells become a closed genus 2 surface.

X = ⋃c ≠ d ec × ed. Equivalently: remove r × r, g × g, and b × b from Θ × Θ.

Counting cells

Θ × Θ has 4 vertices, 12 edges and 9 squares, so χ = 4 − 12 + 9 = 1. Removing the diagonal leaves six squares: χ = 4 − 12 + 6 = −2, which is the Euler characteristic of a genus 2 surface.

The three views

  • Full product: all nine squares; the diagonal cells rr, gg and bb, the ones that will be removed, are shown as ghost tiles.
  • Remove diagonal: the six off-diagonal squares form a closed square complex of genus 2.
  • Genus 2 focus: the same six squares read as the boundary of a thickened theta graph; the two visible tunnels make the genus 2 structure immediate.
