Roots

A Pisot number is a real algebraic integer θ > 1 all of whose Galois conjugates lie strictly inside the unit circle. A Salem number is a real algebraic integer τ > 1 whose conjugates all lie on or inside the unit circle with at least one on it. The plane is shaded by how fast Newton's method z ↦ z − p(z)/p′(z) converges; the basins of the roots tile ℂ in a fractal pattern.

Critical points. Newton's map N = z − p/p′ has N′ = p·p″/p′², so its free critical points are the zeros of p″. Each attracting cycle that is not a root must capture one of them — which is why black (non-converging) regions only appear when a critical orbit gets stuck. Turn on Critical orbits to watch.

Cubic λ-plane. Every cubic is conjugate to pλ(z) = z³ + (λ−1)z − λ = (z − 1)(z² + z + λ), whose only free critical point is 0. Coloring λ by where 0 goes gives the Curry–Garnett–Sullivan picture: black regions are copies of the Mandelbrot set. Click one to open its dynamical plane.

Nova. Adding a constant, z ↦ z − a·p/p′ + c, breaks the roots' fixed points and produces the Nova fractals; the c-plane (seeded at z₀ = 1) is the Nova analogue of the Mandelbrot set.
