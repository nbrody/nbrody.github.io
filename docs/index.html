Visiting Assistant Professor

My research interests lie in geometric group theory and low-dimensional topology. Most of the questions I think about concern the geometry of linear groups in characteristic zero. In other words, I multiply two-by-two matrices for a living.

I received my PhD from UC Berkeley and my undergraduate degree from UC Santa Barbara.

The ping-pong argument

To show that a group is free, look for disjoint subsets of a space that are moved into one another by its generators.

Aⁿ(Y) ⊂ X   and   Bⁿ(X) ⊂ Y,   for n ≠ 0.

Working idea

Use the action on the projective line. Track a point through a reduced word and observe which region contains the result.

QuestionWhich regions make the dynamics easiest to see?

Free subgroups and the
ping-pong lemma

Research notes

1. Introduction. We study a pair of matrices through their action on the projective line. This perspective turns a question about words in a group into one about dynamics.

2. The construction. Let X and Y be disjoint subsets. The ping-pong lemma gives a criterion for freeness.

Teaching

Before that, I was a GSI for more than a dozen courses at UC Berkeley, and a TA at UC Santa Barbara. I have also taught for Mt. Tam College at San Quentin State Prison, and the Project for Inmate Education at Santa Cruz Main Jail.
