A guided tour

The Dynamics of 2-Generator Subgroups of PSL(2,ℂ)

Brooks & Matelski (Stony Brook, 1978; pub. 1981) asked when a discrete two-generator subgroup of \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})\) deforms into an indiscrete one. Their analysis funnels the discreteness question into the iteration of a quadratic polynomial and produces — almost as a byproduct — what is widely cited as the first published image of the Mandelbrot set.

R. Brooks & J. P. Matelski, The Dynamics of 2-Generator Subgroups of PSL(2,ℂ), in Riemann Surfaces and Related Topics (Stony Brook 1978), Ann. of Math. Studies 97, Princeton 1981.

§1 Möbius transformations on the Riemann sphere

An element of \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})\) acts on \(\widehat{\mathbb{C}} = \mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\) by the Möbius transformation \(\displaystyle\gamma(z) = \frac{az+b}{cz+d}\), \(ad-bc=1\). Via stereographic projection this is also a conformal automorphism of the 2-sphere \(S^2\), and extends to an isometry of hyperbolic 3-space \(\mathbb{H}^3\). Brooks & Matelski study finitely generated subgroups of this group acting on \(\mathbb{H}^3\).

§2 Two generators and their limit set

Pick two elements \(A, B \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})\). The group \(\Gamma = \langle A, B\rangle\) is Kleinian when it acts properly discontinuously on \(\mathbb{H}^3\); equivalently when \(\Gamma\) is a discrete subgroup. The limit set \(\Lambda(\Gamma) \subset \widehat{\mathbb{C}}\) is where orbits accumulate on \(\partial\mathbb{H}^3\). Generically it is a Cantor set, a circle, or all of \(\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\).

Following Brooks & Matelski, fix \(A\) parabolic at \(\infty\), say \(A(z) = z + 1\). Then \(\Gamma\) is determined up to conjugation by a complex parameter \(\mu\) appearing in \(B\). We use the Riley-style normal form \(\displaystyle B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -\mu & 1\end{pmatrix}\) so both generators are parabolic and \(\mu \in \mathbb{C}\) is the only continuous datum.

The point cloud is the orbit of \(0\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\) under all words of length \(\le n\) in \(A^{\pm 1}, B^{\pm 1}\). As \(n\to\infty\) it fills out \(\Lambda(\Gamma)\). When \(\mu\) lies in the Riley slice, the group is discrete and free and the limit set is a Cantor circle.

§3 The renormalization trick

Here is the heart of the paper. Suppose \(\Gamma_\mu = \langle A, B_\mu\rangle\) is on the boundary between discrete and indiscrete. Brooks & Matelski study the word \[ W_n = A^{a_n} B_\mu A^{a_{n-1}} B_\mu \cdots A^{a_1} B_\mu \] for cleverly chosen integers \(a_k\). After conjugating by a diagonal matrix that pulls \(W_n\) toward the identity, the recursion on the off-diagonal entry collapses to a one-dimensional iteration \[ z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + c, \qquad c = c(\mu). \] The complex constant \(c\) depends polynomially on \(\mu\). Discreteness of \(\Gamma_\mu\) translates, after this reduction, into the orbit \(0, c, c^2+c, \ldots\) staying bounded.

Below: the moving frame. The black grid is the original picture in coordinates adapted to \(A\). After successive renormalizations \(z \mapsto z^2 + c\), the picture re-coordinates itself; the limit pattern is the Julia set for \(c\). Drag the slider to step through the renormalization tower.

§4 Iteration of \(z \mapsto z^2 + c\)

Now we forget the group and watch the orbit of \(0\) under \(f_c(z) = z^2 + c\). Brooks & Matelski observed that the set \[ M \;=\; \bigl\{\, c\in\mathbb{C} \;:\; \text{the orbit }0,\,c,\,c^2+c,\,\ldots\text{ is bounded}\,\bigr\} \] controls discreteness in their family. Today we call \(M\) the Mandelbrot set.

Below, the three-dimensional plot shows the orbit \(z_0=0,\,z_1,\,z_2,\ldots\) lifted by step number on the vertical axis. Drag \(c\) on the left plane and the orbit re-draws live. When \(c \in M\) the curve stays within a bounded vertical cylinder; otherwise it spirals off to infinity.

§6 Julia sets at a glance

For each \(c\), the filled Julia set \(K_c\) is the set of starting points \(z\) whose orbit under \(f_c\) stays bounded. The dichotomy \(0 \in M \Leftrightarrow K_c\) is connected goes back to Fatou–Julia. Brooks & Matelski's discreteness criterion is parallel: the relevant group is discrete iff a certain orbit is bounded, and the parameter locus is \(M\).

§7 Closing the loop

We started with two parabolic Möbius elements and a question — when is the group they generate discrete? After conjugating, the answer reduced to whether a single complex parameter \(c\) lies in \(M\). The figure above is the answer set, drawn directly out of group theory.

The same scaffolding — fix one generator, vary the other, track a renormalized iterate — recurs across Kleinian-group theory: it is what produces the Riley slice, the Maskit slice, and the general theory of holomorphic families of representations. The Mandelbrot picture sits at one corner of that broader landscape.
