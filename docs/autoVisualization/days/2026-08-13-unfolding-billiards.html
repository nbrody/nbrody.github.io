Unfolding Billiards in Polygons

Rather than reflect the ball at a wall, reflect the table: the Katok–Zemlyakov unfolding straightens a billiard trajectory into a single straight line threading a chain of mirrored copies of the polygon. If every angle is a rational multiple of π, say miπ/ni with N = lcm(ni), the direction takes only 2N values and the copies glue into a compact translation surface of genus g = 1 + (N/2) Σ(mi−1)/ni. For the five kaleidoscopic tables here that surface is the torus ℝ²/L, where L is the translation lattice of the reflection group generated by the walls (computed below by exhausting the group), so the billiard flow becomes linear flow on a torus and a trajectory is periodic exactly when its direction is parallel to a vector of L — for the square, the classical statement that a trajectory closes iff its slope p/q is rational, with period 2√(p²+q²) and 2(p+q) bounces. Every other direction is dense and equidistributed, by unique ergodicity of irrational torus flow.
