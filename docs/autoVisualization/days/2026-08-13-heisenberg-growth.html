Polynomial Growth of the Heisenberg Group

The integer Heisenberg group H = H3(ℤ) of upper unitriangular 3×3 matrices is nilpotent of class 2, so like ℤ³ it has polynomial growth — but not of the dimension you would guess. Bass and Guivarc'h computed the degree exactly: |B(R)| ≈ c Rd with d = Σk≥1 k · rank(γk/γk+1), where γk is the lower central series. For H the quotients are ℤ² and ℤ, giving d = 1·2 + 2·1 = 4 even though H is a 3-dimensional group: the commutator direction z = [x,y] is reached only at quadratic cost, because a word's z-coordinate is the signed area Σ x Δy swept by its projected path, and area grows like length². Both balls below are computed by exact breadth-first search on the Cayley graph; the log–log plot fits the exponent live, and it stays 4 no matter which finite generating set you choose.
