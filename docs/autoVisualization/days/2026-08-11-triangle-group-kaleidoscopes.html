Triangle-Group Kaleidoscopes

Fix integers p, q, r ≥ 2 and a geodesic triangle ABC with angles π/p, π/q, π/r. Poincaré's theorem for reflection groups says the reflections a, b, c in the sides opposite A, B, C generate a discrete group ⟨a,b,c | a²=b²=c²=(bc)p=(ca)q=(ab)r=1⟩ with that triangle as a strict fundamental domain, so its images tile the whole space exactly once each. The sign of 1/p + 1/q + 1/r − 1 decides the space: positive gives the round sphere, where the group is finite of order 4/(1/p+1/q+1/r−1); zero gives the Euclidean plane; negative gives the hyperbolic plane, where Gauss–Bonnet makes the triangle's area exactly the angle defect π(1 − 1/p − 1/q − 1/r).
