Dehn Twists and the Mapping Class Group of the Torus

Acting on H1(T²;ℤ) = ℤ² gives an isomorphism Mod(T²) ≅ SL2(ℤ), and a Dehn twist about a curve c acts by the Picard–Lefschetz formula x ↦ x + ⟨c,x⟩c: the twists about the meridian a = (1,0) and longitude b = (0,1) become [[1,1],[0,1]] and [[1,0],[−1,1]], which generate SL2(ℤ) subject to the braid relation TaTbTa = TbTaTb and (TaTb)6 = 1. Essential simple closed curves up to isotopy are the primitive classes ±(p,q), i.e. slopes q/p ∈ ℚ ∪ {∞}, with geometric intersection number |ps − qr|; and the Nielsen–Thurston type of a mapping class is read off its trace — |tr| < 2 periodic, |tr| = 2 reducible (or ±I), |tr| > 2 Anosov, stretching its unstable foliation by λ = (|tr| + √(tr² − 4))/2.
