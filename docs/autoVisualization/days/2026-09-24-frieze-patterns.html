Conway–Coxeter Frieze Patterns

A frieze pattern of width n−3 is a staggered array of positive integers between a row of 1s and a row of 1s in which every diamond b over a d over c satisfies the SL₂ rule ad − bc = 1. Conway and Coxeter (1973) proved that such friezes are in bijection with triangulations of a convex n-gon: the first nontrivial row is the quiddity (a0, …, an−1), where av is the number of triangles at vertex v, and the rest is forced by mi,j+1 = aj mi,j − mi,j−1. The entry mi,j is the Ptolemy (λ-)length of the chord ij when every triangulation edge has length 1, so the interior 1s are exactly the diagonals, the whole pattern is invariant under a glide reflection mi,j = mj,i+n of period n, and a diagonal flip is a cluster mutation.
