The Rauzy Fractal of the Tribonacci Substitution

The substitution σ: a→ab, b→ac, c→a has incidence matrix M with characteristic polynomial x³ − x² − x − 1, whose Perron root is the Tribonacci constant β = 1.8392867552…; the other two roots are complex conjugates of modulus β−1/2, so β is a Pisot number and M contracts the plane they span. Write the fixed point u = abacabaabacababacaba… as a staircase in ℤ³ — step by ea, eb or ec per letter — and project each vertex along the expanding eigendirection into that contracting plane by φ(x) = ⟨x, wα⟩ ∈ ℂ, where wαM = αwα: the closure of the resulting bounded point set is Rauzy's fractal R, cut into three subtiles Ra, Rb, Rc by the letter that follows the prefix. Rauzy (1982) proved that R tiles the plane by the lattice Γ = φ({x ∈ ℤ³ : Σxi = 0}) and that the exchange of the three subtiles, x ↦ x + φ(ei) on Ri, is conjugate to a rotation of the torus ℂ/Γ — so the symbolic dynamics of σ is measurably a two-dimensional irrational rotation, and following one prefix point forward is exactly reading off u.
