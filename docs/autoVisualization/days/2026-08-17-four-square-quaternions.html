Jacobi's Four-Square Theorem, Quaternionically

Lagrange proved every non-negative integer is a sum of four integer squares; Jacobi made it quantitative: the number of ordered representations is r4(n) = 8 ∑d|n, 4 ∤ d d, the coefficient identity θ(q)4 = 1 + 8 ∑ (that divisor sum) qn for a weight-2 modular form on Γ0(4). Those representations are the Lipschitz quaternions a+bi+cj+dk ∈ ℤ4 of norm n, and that set is closed under left multiplication by the unit group {±1,±i,±j,±k}; since ℍ is a division ring the eight multiples of a nonzero quaternion are always distinct, so every orbit has size exactly 8 — the 8 in Jacobi's formula is the order of the unit group, and the divisor sum counts the orbits. Enlarge ℤ4 to the Hurwitz order ℤ4 ∪ (ℤ+½)4 — the D4 lattice, a maximal order whose unit group is the 24 vertices of the 24-cell — and the same argument turns Jacobi's theorem into 24 ∑d|n, d odd d. Each representation is drawn as a point of the sphere of radius √n, normalised to S3 and projected to space; the motion is left multiplication by eiθ, an isometry of S3 (the Hopf flow), which at θ = π/2 is multiplication by i and therefore permutes the points — the whole configuration returns to itself four times per turn.
