Lagrange–Gauss Lattice Reduction

Given a basis (b1, b2) of a lattice Λ ⊂ ℝ², swap so that ‖b1‖ ≤ ‖b2‖ and then subtract the nearest integer multiple, b2 ← b2 − ⌈⟨b1,b2⟩/⟨b1,b1⟩⌋ b1, and repeat: this two-dimensional Euclidean algorithm terminates, and Lagrange's theorem says the output is reduced — ‖b1‖ ≤ ‖b2‖ and 2|⟨b1,b2⟩| ≤ ‖b1‖² — which forces ‖b1‖ = λ1 and ‖b2‖ = λ2, the successive minima of Λ. Each step is an elementary matrix, so the whole run factors the change of basis into the generators S = [[0,−1],[1,0]] and T = [[1,1],[0,1]] of SL2(ℤ). Writing τ = b2/b1 in ℂ, the two reduction conditions read exactly |τ| ≥ 1 and |Re τ| ≤ ½: the algorithm is the descent of τ into the standard fundamental domain of SL2(ℤ) acting on ℍ, drawn at right. Since covol(Λ) = ‖b1‖² Im τ, the reduced basis satisfies λ1²/covol(Λ) = 1/Im τ ≤ 2/√3 = γ2, Hermite's constant, with equality only at the corner τ = eiπ/3 — the hexagonal lattice.
