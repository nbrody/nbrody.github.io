Kesten's Criterion: Random Walks and Amenability

Let G be generated by a finite symmetric set S, |S| = d, and let the simple random walk step by a uniform element of S. The return probability p2n(e) is the number of words of length 2n in S that equal the identity, divided by d2n; since a loop of length 2n followed by a loop of length 2m is a loop, p2n+2m ≥ p2np2m, so by Fekete's lemma p2n(e)1/2n increases to a limit ρ — the norm of the Markov operator on ℓ²(G). Kesten's theorem (1959): ρ = 1 if and only if G is amenable, and for the d-regular tree — the Cayley graph of Fk with d = 2k, or of a free product of d involutions — ρ = 2√(d−1)/d. So ℤ² and the integer Heisenberg group sit at ρ = 1 while F2 is pinned at √3/2 = 0.8660… Every count below is computed exactly (BigInt binomials, an exact transfer recursion on the tree, and a pruned integer dynamic program on H3(ℤ)), and cross-checked against brute-force enumeration of all dm words for m ≤ 8.
