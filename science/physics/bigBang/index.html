Big Bang & Cosmic Web

Start from the singularity: a hot, dense point detonates and matter streams outward into an expanding universe. As it cools, gravity takes over — tiny density ripples grow, and the dust drains out of the voids to settle into the filaments and clusters of the cosmic web.

How it works

Thousands of mass tracers are born in a tiny, hot region and flung outward with a Hubble-like velocity, filling a periodic (wrap-around) universe. Gravity is solved on a grid with a particle–mesh scheme: matter is deposited into cells, the density field is smoothed to a softened potential, and each particle is accelerated down its gradient toward over-dense regions. An expansion-drag term damps peculiar velocities so structure can freeze out — letting the initial ripples collapse into sheets, filaments, and clusters: the cosmic web. Color tracks blackbody-style temperature (hot blue → cool red, with cluster cores reheating), local density, or speed.
